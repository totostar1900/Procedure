## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This plan sets out what Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company") does to continue serving its clients when an event disrupts its business, and to return to normal as quickly as possible.

It has three aims, in this order:

1. **protect people**: Staff Members, clients and visitors;
2. **protect clients**: their orders, their assets, their data and their information;
3. **protect the Company**: its obligations towards COSUMAF and the market, its data and its reputation.

### Article 2 — A plan tailored to the Company

The Company is a small team whose tools are, for the most part, accessible online. Its Staff Members can work remotely. The plan draws three consequences from this:

- **the office is not indispensable**: the default response to most disruptions is to continue working remotely;
- **people are the main point of fragility**: each key role has a trained deputy with access rights ready for use;
- **the plan must remain short and usable under pressure**: a crisis checklist (Appendix 1), a table of essential activities (Appendix 2) and a directory (Appendix 3) are enough to act.

### Article 3 — Definitions

- **Disruption**: an event that prevents or slows down an activity of the Company, whatever its cause.
- **Crisis**: a disruption that threatens an essential activity beyond its recovery time, affects several clients or may affect the Company's reputation.
- **Essential activity**: an activity whose interruption would quickly cause harm to clients or a regulatory breach.
- **Maximum tolerable period of disruption**: the period beyond which the interruption of an essential activity becomes unacceptable.
- **Maximum tolerable data loss**: the maximum amount of information that the Company accepts it may have to reconstruct after an incident, expressed as a period of time.
- **Degraded mode**: a temporary, slower or more manual way of working that allows an essential activity to continue.

## Title II — What must be protected

### Article 4 — Essential activities and recovery times

The following table sets the maximum tolerable period of disruption for each essential activity. The fallback solutions and resources required are set out in Appendix 2.

| Activity | Maximum tolerable period of disruption | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Informing clients of a disruption and telling them how to contact the Company | 2 business hours | Clients must know whom to contact and must not act blindly |
| Receiving, time-stamping and transmitting client orders | Half a trading day | An order that is not transmitted may cause the client a loss |
| Monitoring settlement and responding to calls from BVMAC and the Central Securities Depository | 1 business day | A settlement failure engages the Company's liability towards the market |
| Enabling clients to know their assets and to dispose of them | 1 business day | The assets belong to the clients |
| Payments to clients and account reconciliations | 2 business days | Avoiding payment errors and delays |
| AML/CFT due diligence and sanctions screening | Before any new transaction concerned | No client onboarding or unusual transaction without the checks; they are suspended rather than carried out without checks |
| Reports to COSUMAF, ANIF and the authorities | According to the regulatory deadline | A missed deadline is a breach |
| Advice, fund-raising transactions, accounting, payroll | 5 business days | Important activities, but ones that can wait a few days |

### Article 5 — Maximum tolerable data loss

- **Client orders and instructions: no loss.** Every order is recorded as soon as it is received. If systems are unavailable, it is entered in the backup order log in Appendix 4 and then input as soon as normal operations resume.
- **Documents and email: one day at most.** Backups are made at least daily.
- **Accounting and client files: one day at most.**

### Article 6 — What essential activities depend on

Essential activities rely on a small number of elements, which are protected as a priority:

- **people**: the Chief Executive Officer, the RCCI, the Head of Operations, and their deputies;
- **online tools**: email and documents, client and order management tools, access to the systems of BVMAC and the Central Securities Depository, online banking;
- laptop **workstations** and business **telephones**;
- **electricity** and **internet access**;
- **critical service providers**, monitored in accordance with the Outsourcing Policy (PC-POL-EXT-001);
- **market infrastructures and banks**.

## Title III — Planned scenarios

### Article 7 — Principle

The plan does not attempt to foresee every event. It plans for **consequences**: the office can no longer be used, there is no electricity or internet, a person is missing, data is affected, a partner fails. Whatever the cause, the response is the same.

### Article 8 — The office is inaccessible

Fire, water damage, flooding, building works, unrest in the neighbourhood, administrative closure or simply being unable to get there.

- Everyone works from home or another safe location, using their laptop and business telephone.
- Client meetings are held by video conference, by telephone or at the temporary fallback location specified in Appendix 3.
- If the office remains unavailable for more than two weeks, the Chief Executive Officer decides on a temporary premises solution.

### Article 9 — Power outage

- **At the office**: a generator takes over. The fuel reserve allows at least one day's work; start-up is checked every month and maintenance is followed up according to the supplier's logbook. Switching to the generator causes a brief outage: laptops continue on battery power, and the IT lead makes sure the internet connection restarts.
- **At home**: everyone keeps their telephone and laptop charged whenever power is available. If the outage prevents work, the Staff Member goes to the office or another location with power.
- If the generator breaks down or runs out of fuel, Staff Members switch to remote working from a location with power.

### Article 10 — Internet outage

Outages may be local (operator, neighbourhood cable) or national (incident on a submarine cable, network restrictions).

- **Local outage**: the office has two independent connections, with two different operators. Each Staff Member has a mobile data plan with an operator different from the office's and can share his or her telephone's connection.
- **National or very widespread outage**: voice calls and SMS often still work. The Company informs clients by SMS, receives their orders by telephone in accordance with Article 18, records them in the backup order log and applies the fallback procedure agreed with BVMAC. It does not use any means of connection that is not authorised in Cameroon.

### Article 11 — A key person is absent

Illness, accident, travel, epidemic, sudden resignation.

- Each key role has a **designated deputy** listed in Appendix 2, trained and given the necessary access rights before they are needed.
- Bank signing authorities and authorisations with BVMAC and the Central Securities Depository are organised so that no transaction depends on a single person.
- Passwords for shared accounts and emergency accounts are kept in a digital vault, with emergency access placed under the responsibility of the Chief Executive Officer and the Chairman of the Board of Directors.
- Essential procedures are written down: a competent person must be able to follow them without prior training.

### Article 12 — Cyberattack or data loss

Ransomware, email hacking, theft or loss of a computer, accidental deletion.

1. **Isolate**: disconnect the affected device from the network, without switching it off, and immediately notify the IT lead and the IT service provider.
2. **Block**: change the passwords concerned and close open sessions, from an uncompromised device.
3. **Prevent fraud**: alert the banks and remind Staff Members that no payment or instruction received by email or messaging is executed without confirmation through another channel.
4. **Restore** from a clean backup, preferably the copy kept offline.
5. **Inform**: if client data is affected, the crisis unit informs the clients concerned and the data protection authority under the conditions laid down by law, and COSUMAF where the incident is significant.

The Company does not pay ransoms.

### Article 13 — Failure of a service provider

The IT service provider, the online email service or another critical service provider no longer provides its service.

- The crisis unit contacts the service provider, obtains a restoration time and informs Staff Members accordingly.
- If that time exceeds the time set for the essential activity concerned, it applies the fallback solution in Appendix 2 and then, if necessary, the exit strategy provided for in the Outsourcing Policy.

### Article 14 — Failure of a market infrastructure or a bank

BVMAC, the Central Securities Depository or a settlement bank interrupts its service.

- The Company follows the infrastructure's instructions and never seeks a solution outside the market rules.
- It informs its clients that orders are retained and will be transmitted as soon as service resumes, or offers to cancel them.
- It keeps evidence of the date and time of each order received during the interruption.

### Article 15 — Large-scale event

Serious unrest, natural disaster, epidemic, restrictions on movement.

- The safety of people comes first: no one travels to the office or to a client if doing so presents a risk.
- The Company switches to remote working and reduces its business to the essential activities in Article 4.
- The crisis unit checks in with each Staff Member at least once a day.

## Title IV — What is permanently in place

### Article 16 — Preventive measures

Continuity is prepared before the crisis. At all times:

1. **Workstations**: each Staff Member has an encrypted business laptop and a business telephone. Working documents are saved in the Company's online storage space, not only on the workstation.
2. **Access**: multi-factor authentication on all accounts, with a registered backup second authentication method.
3. **Backups**: at least three copies of important data, on two different media, one of which is kept offline or with a provider other than the main one. Restoration is tested every quarter.
4. **Connectivity and power**: in accordance with Articles 9 and 10.
5. **Crisis file**: the directory in Appendix 3, the crisis checklist in Appendix 1, the backup order log in Appendix 4, and a copy of the Company's key documents (licence, articles of association, bank signing authorities, critical contracts, insurance policies). It is available online, on an encrypted USB key and in paper form with each member of the crisis unit.
6. **Service providers**: the contracts of critical service providers include continuity commitments, in accordance with the Outsourcing Policy.
7. **Insurance**: each year the Company examines whether its policies cover damage to premises and equipment, business interruption and IT incidents.

### Article 17 — Remote working

Remote working is a normal way of operating, not merely a fallback mode. It is subject to the following rules:

- only business equipment is used for client data;
- public Wi-Fi networks are not used; the telephone's shared connection is used instead;
- confidential conversations are held out of earshot of others and the screen is not visible to third parties;
- paper documents containing client data are not taken home unless necessary, and are kept under lock and key there;
- each Staff Member remains reachable during working hours and reports any connection difficulty without delay.

### Article 18 — Receiving orders in degraded mode

When the usual channels are unavailable:

- an order received by telephone is accepted only after the client has been identified in accordance with the usual procedure and, for a large amount, after calling the client back on the number recorded in his or her file;
- every order is immediately recorded in the backup order log in Appendix 4, with the date and time of receipt, and then confirmed to the client in writing as soon as possible;
- an order received through a personal messaging service or an unusual channel is executed only after the same verification;
- any request for a transfer to a new or modified account is refused until the situation has returned to normal, unless enhanced verification is carried out by the RCCI.

### Article 19 — Client access to their assets

Clients' securities are recorded with the Central Securities Depository or with their account keeper, and their funds are deposited in accounts separate from those of the Company. Even if the Company could no longer continue its business, clients retain their assets.

In Appendix 1, the plan provides for the message to be sent to clients, in liaison with COSUMAF, telling them how to access their assets should the Company have to cease its business permanently.

## Title V — Crisis organisation

### Article 20 — Crisis unit

The crisis unit is deliberately small:

| Role | Holder | Deputy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crisis lead: decides, coordinates, speaks on behalf of the Company | Chief Executive Officer | RCCI |
| Clients, market and operations | Head of Operations | A designated Staff Member from the sales department |
| Compliance, authorities, data security, crisis log | RCCI | Chief Executive Officer |
| IT and service providers | In-house IT lead | IT service provider |

The Chairman of the Board of Directors is informed as soon as the unit is activated and receives an update at least daily for as long as the crisis lasts.

### Article 21 — Activation

1. **Any Staff Member** who observes a disruption immediately notifies the Chief Executive Officer or, failing that, the RCCI, by telephone.
2. **The crisis lead assesses** the situation within the hour: which activities are affected, for how long, and with what effect on clients.
3. **The crisis lead decides**:
   - **incident**: handled by the persons concerned and reported in accordance with the Risk Management Policy;
   - **disruption**: switch to degraded mode for the activities affected;
   - **crisis**: activation of the crisis unit and of this plan.

When in doubt, the crisis lead activates the crisis unit: it is easier to stand it down than to make up for lost time.

### Article 22 — Crisis management

The crisis unit follows the crisis checklist in Appendix 1. It:

- makes sure of the safety and availability of each Staff Member;
- decides which activities to maintain, suspend or postpone;
- keeps a **crisis log**: decisions, time, author, information received and passed on;
- meets at set times, remotely if necessary;
- decides on the return to normal.

### Article 23 — Communication

| Recipient | Who | When | How |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Staff Members | Crisis lead | Upon activation, then at each update | Telephone call following the call tree, then messaging group; SMS if the internet is down |
| Clients | Head of Operations | Within 2 business hours if clients are affected | SMS, email, message on the website and on the telephone line |
| COSUMAF | RCCI, on the decision of the crisis lead | Without delay for any disruption affecting clients or the market | Telephone, then in writing |
| BVMAC, Central Securities Depository, banks | Head of Operations | As soon as a transaction in progress is at risk | In accordance with their procedures |
| Data protection authority | RCCI | In the event of a personal data breach, within the statutory deadline | In writing |
| Press and public | Chief Executive Officer only | If necessary | Approved written statement |

Messages to clients are factual and brief, and always state what the client must do and how to contact the Company. Templates are provided in Appendix 1.

### Article 24 — Return to normal and lessons learned

Before declaring the end of the crisis, the unit checks that:

- orders recorded in degraded mode have been input and reconciled;
- restored data is complete;
- the clients and authorities that were informed have received an end-of-disruption message.

Within 30 days, the RCCI prepares a lessons-learned review using Appendix 5: what happened, what worked, what needs to change. The plan is updated accordingly.

## Title VI — Keeping the plan alive

### Article 25 — Exercises

| Exercise | Frequency | What is checked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Call tree | Every six months | Everyone can be reached in less than one hour; the directory is up to date |
| Remote working day for the whole team | Every quarter | All tools and essential activities work outside the office |
| Restoration of a backup | Every quarter | Data can be recovered complete and readable |
| Replacement of a key person | Once a year | The deputy carries out the holder's essential operations unaided |
| Tabletop exercise, based on a scenario from Title III | Once a year | The unit is able to decide and communicate; the times in Article 4 can be met |

Each exercise gives rise to a form in Appendix 5. Shortcomings identified are followed up as action plans.

### Article 26 — Training

Each new Staff Member receives this plan on arrival and takes part in the next exercise. Members of the crisis unit and their deputies are familiar with the crisis checklist and know where to find the crisis file.

### Article 27 — Updating

The directory in Appendix 3 is checked every quarter. The plan is reviewed at least once a year, and upon any significant change: new premises, new critical tool or service provider, new activity, departure of a key person, actual crisis.

Each year the RCCI presents to the Board of Directors a review of exercises, incidents and updates. This review is included in the annual internal control report sent to COSUMAF.

## Title VII — Final provisions

### Article 28 — Relationship with other documents

This plan supersedes the minimum requirements of Article 20 of the Risk Management Policy. It applies together with the Outsourcing Policy and, once adopted, the Information Systems Security Policy.

### Article 29 — Entry into force

This plan enters into force on the date of its adoption by the Board of Directors, as indicated in the document control sheet.
