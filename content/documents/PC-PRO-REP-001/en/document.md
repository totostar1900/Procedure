## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This procedure organises all the submissions that Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company") must make to the authorities and to market bodies. It sets out what to send, to whom, when, who prepares it, who checks it, who signs it, and how to keep evidence of the submission.

It puts into practice the compliance calendar provided for by the Internal Control and Compliance Charter (PC-COD-003).

### Article 2 — Why it matters

A late document is costly and visible: the COSUMAF General Regulation provides for a penalty of **FCFA 250,000 per day of delay**, counted until actual submission, and higher amounts where an injunction has been issued or for certain transactions. Failure to submit the annual AML/CFT report exposes the Company to a sanction of at least FCFA 10,000,000. Brokerage firms in the sub-region have lost their licence, in particular for failing to submit their internal control reports.

A false or inconsistent document is even more serious than a late one: it engages the liability of the senior managers who signed it.

### Article 3 — Scope

The procedure covers submissions:

- to **COSUMAF**: annual reports, periodic returns, financial statements, applications for authorisation, event notifications, authorisations of staff;
- to **ANIF**: suspicious transaction reports and reports required by instruction;
- to **BVMAC** and the **central securities depository**: member and participant obligations;
- to **primary dealers in government securities** and to **BEAC**, as regards government securities;
- to the commercial court **registry**: filing of the approved financial statements.

Tax and social security returns are the responsibility of accounting and are not covered here, except where a market authority requests a copy.

### Article 4 — Who does what

| Function | Role |
| --- | --- |
| RCCI | Maintains the calendar (Appendix 1) and the register of submissions (Appendix 3); prepares the internal control and AML/CFT reports; checks the returns prepared by others; submits; is the usual point of contact for COSUMAF; is the only person authorised to send a suspicious transaction report to ANIF |
| Accounting | Prepares the financial statements and quantitative returns; provides data to the RCCI |
| Desk (operations) and Capital Markets Department | Provide activity, client and market data; prepare the returns due to BVMAC and the central securities depository |
| Chief Executive Officer | Approves and signs submissions that bind the Company; decides on event notifications; applies for prior authorisations |
| Board of Directors | Reviews the annual internal control report before it is submitted; approves the accounts |

The person who prepares a return is never the person who checks it. Where the RCCI prepares a report personally, the Chief Executive Officer acts as second reviewer.

## Title II — The calendar

### Article 5 — Annual calendar

In December, the RCCI draws up the calendar of submissions for the following year in accordance with Appendix 1, showing for each submission the legal deadline and an **internal date** set at least five business days earlier. The RCCI presents it to the risk and compliance committee.

Deadlines set by the COSUMAF General Regulation are certain. Those arising from instructions or practice are marked "to be confirmed" until verified against the text or with the body concerned.

### Article 6 — Monthly monitoring

Each month, the risk and compliance committee reviews the submissions of the past month and those due in the next two months. Any deadline at risk is reported to the Chief Executive Officer.

## Title III — The workflow for a periodic submission

### Article 7 — Preparation

The preparer designated in Appendix 1 draws up the return or report from the Company's data: accounting, the order register and cash journal of the Guichet, compliance registers. The preparer keeps the preparation file: sources, extractions, calculations. The preparer reports any missing or doubtful data without waiting for the internal date.

### Article 8 — Four-eyes check

A second reviewer checks the return using the form in Appendix 2:

- completeness against the applicable template or instruction;
- consistency of the figures with the accounts, reconciliations and previous returns;
- consistency between the various returns submitted;
- explanation of significant variations;
- compliance with the required format and method of submission.

Discrepancies are corrected before approval.

### Article 9 — Approval and signature

The Chief Executive Officer approves and signs the return. The annual internal control report is presented to the Board of Directors before it is submitted, in accordance with the Internal Control and Compliance Charter; the Board may attach its observations without altering the findings.

### Article 10 — Submission and evidence

The RCCI submits the return using the method required by the recipient: delivery against receipt, email to the official address, online platform. The RCCI keeps evidence of submission and the acknowledgement of receipt, and records the submission in the register in Appendix 3 on the same day.

If no acknowledgement of receipt is received within five business days, the RCCI follows up with the recipient and keeps a record of the follow-up.

### Article 11 — Archiving

The submitted return, its approval form, the preparation file, the evidence of submission and the acknowledgement of receipt are filed together and kept for **ten years**.

## Title IV — Event-driven submissions

### Article 12 — Prior authorisations

Certain decisions may be implemented only after authorisation or approval by COSUMAF: any change to the licence file or to the senior managers' file, any change to the programme of operations, the appointment of a statutory auditor. The RCCI is consulted from the planning stage; the Chief Executive Officer files the application before any implementation. None of these decisions is carried out before COSUMAF has responded.

Changes to the articles of association, share capital, shareholding and registered office are, as a precaution, handled in the same way until COSUMAF has confirmed that simply informing it is sufficient.

### Article 13 — Notifications without delay

The events listed in Appendix 4 are brought to the attention of the recipient indicated **without delay**, on the decision of the Chief Executive Officer or, failing that, of the Chairman of the Board of Directors. The RCCI prepares the notification. An initial notice by telephone or email is confirmed in writing within 48 hours.

### Article 14 — Reports to ANIF

Suspicious transaction reports are sent to ANIF by the AML/CFT officer, without delay, in accordance with the AML/CFT Policy (PC-POL-LBC-001). They do not follow the approval workflow in Article 9: no one may delay or prevent a report. They are recorded in a confidential register separate from that in Appendix 3.

## Title V — Delays and errors

### Article 15 — Foreseeable delay

As soon as a delay becomes likely, the preparer alerts the RCCI, who informs the Chief Executive Officer. If the delay cannot be avoided, the Company writes to the recipient **before the deadline** to explain the cause and state the date on which the document will be submitted. This step does not suspend penalties, but it demonstrates the Company's good faith.

Any delay is reported as an incident in accordance with the Risk Management Policy.

### Article 16 — Error discovered after submission

An error discovered in a return already submitted is reported to the Chief Executive Officer. The Company sends the recipient, without waiting for the next deadline, a corrected return accompanied by a note explaining the error and its correction. The original return is never destroyed: it is kept together with the correction.

### Article 17 — Monitoring

Each quarter, the RCCI reports to the Board of Directors on submissions made, delays and corrections. Compliance with this procedure forms part of the annual control plan and of the independent review.

### Article 18 — Entry into force

This procedure enters into force on the date of its approval, as shown in the document control sheet. The calendar in Appendix 1 is updated whenever the authorities issue a new instruction.
