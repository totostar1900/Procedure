## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This charter sets out **who does what** at Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company"): which bodies govern it, how it is organised, what each function must do, what one person may not combine, who replaces whom in case of absence, and who may commit the Company, up to what amount and with what second signature.

It is the first chapter of the procedures manual. The other documents in the repository rely on it when they designate a function.

### Article 2 — Scope

The charter applies to directors, senior managers, all Staff Members, whatever their contract and length of service, and to service providers performing a function on behalf of the Company, to the extent provided for in their contract.

### Article 3 — Definitions and cross-references

- **Senior manager (dirigeant effectif)**: a person who effectively determines the direction of the Company's business and whose appointment is subject to the prior authorisation of COSUMAF. The Company has at least two.
- **Delegation of authority**: a written instrument by which a person holding a power entrusts part of it, for a specified purpose, amount and duration, to another person who accepts it.
- **Delegation of signature**: a delegation that allows documents of a given type to be signed on behalf of the Company, without transferring the decision itself.
- **Four eyes**: the rule under which a transaction is prepared by one person and checked or validated by another.
- **Back-up (interim)**: temporary replacement of an absent holder by a person designated in advance.

The charter does not repeat rules set elsewhere; it refers to them:

| Subject | Document |
| --- | --- |
| Functioning of the Board of Directors, role of its Chairman, Board committees | Board Internal Rules (PC-COD-002) |
| Internal control, three lines, status and independence of the RCCI | Internal Control and Compliance Charter (PC-COD-003) |
| Remuneration of senior managers and Staff Members | Remuneration Policy (PC-POL-REM-001) |
| Drafting, approval and updating of documents | Document Control Procedure (PC-PRO-GOV-001) |

### Article 4 — Principles

1. **Clarity**: each important task has a person responsible, designated by their function, and a deputy.
2. **Separation**: whoever decides or executes is not the one who controls.
3. **In writing**: every delegated power is in writing, entered in the register and known to the persons concerned.
4. **Proportionality**: the Company has few people; it combines functions where the regulations allow, and offsets each combination with a verifiable measure.
5. **Continuity**: no absence blocks a decision or removes a control.

## Title II — Governance bodies

### Article 5 — General meeting of shareholders

The general meeting approves the accounts, appoints and dismisses the directors and the statutory auditors, sets the directors' fees and decides on amendments to the articles of association and to the share capital, under the conditions of the OHADA Uniform Act and the articles of association. Any decision that modifies the licence file is first submitted to COSUMAF (Article 34).

### Article 6 — Board of Directors

The Board sets the Company's strategic direction, appoints and dismisses the Chairman and the Chief Executive Officer, sets their powers, approves the codes and policies and oversees risks and internal control. Its duties and functioning are set out in PC-COD-002.

Under this charter, the Board:

- approves the organisation chart in Appendix 1 and the delegation matrix in Appendix 2;
- designates the second senior manager (Article 9);
- approves the list of bank signatories and their categories (Article 27);
- authorises transactions that exceed the powers of the Chief Executive Officer.

### Article 7 — Chairman of the Board of Directors

The Chairman organises and leads the work of the Board (PC-COD-002, Title IV). He does not manage the Company and has no line authority over Staff Members. He is the second level of control over the acts of the Chief Executive Officer: he receives the RCCI's alerts directly, approves the expense claims and personal expenses of the Chief Executive Officer, and gives his prior agreement in the cases set out in Appendix 2.

### Article 8 — Chief Executive Officer

The Chief Executive Officer manages the Company and represents it in dealings with third parties. He has the powers conferred by law and the articles of association, within the internal limits set by the Board and restated in Appendix 2.

These limits bind the Chief Executive Officer towards the Company; they cannot be enforced against third parties. Any overstepping is brought to the attention of the Chairman of the Board without delay and regularised at the next meeting of the Board.

The Chief Executive Officer is a senior manager (dirigeant effectif). The functions of Chairman of the Board and Chief Executive Officer are held by two different persons (PC-COD-002, Article 7).

### Article 9 — Second senior manager

The Company has at all times a second senior manager, recognised by COSUMAF. The Board designates this person on the proposal of the Chief Executive Officer:

- either a **Deputy Chief Executive Officer**, appointed by the Board under the conditions of the Uniform Act and the articles of association;
- or, failing that, a **head of department** of the Company, preferably the Head of Capital Markets, granted by the Board a general delegation to stand in for the Chief Executive Officer.

The second senior manager acts as back-up for the Chief Executive Officer, is a category A co-signatory on the bank accounts and takes part in all decisions that require two senior managers under Appendix 2. This person is not chosen from among the holders of a control function.

The appointment is implemented only after authorisation by COSUMAF (Article 34).

## Title III — Organisation

### Article 10 — Organisation chart and reporting lines

The organisation chart in Appendix 1 shows, for each function, its line reporting, any functional link, the current holder designated by function only, combinations and the deputy.

All heads of department, the RCCI, the operations officer and the accountant report to the Chief Executive Officer. The client relationship officer reports to the Head of Sales.

The organisation chart is kept up to date by the Chief Executive Officer. Any change is approved by the Board at its next meeting and circulated to all Staff Members within five business days.

### Article 11 — Independence of control functions

The RCCI reports to the Chief Executive Officer. The RCCI has **direct access**, without referring to anyone, to the Chairman of the Board of Directors and to the Board, and, where the regulations so provide, to COSUMAF. The RCCI's status, rights and guarantees of independence are set out in PC-COD-003 (Articles 9 to 13).

Accordingly:

- the RCCI holds no delegation of banking, commercial or commitment signature;
- no delegation entrusts the RCCI with performing a task the RCCI controls;
- the RCCI's appointment, sanction and dismissal follow PC-COD-003; the RCCI's remuneration follows PC-POL-REM-001.

### Article 12 — Outsourced functions

Accounting, IT, the annual independent review and, where applicable, legal advice may be entrusted to service providers in accordance with the Outsourcing Policy (PC-POL-EXT-001). Each service has an **internal owner** designated in Appendix 1, who monitors the contract, checks the quality of service and reports on it. A service provider never receives signing authority on behalf of the Company.

## Title IV — Job descriptions

### Article 13 — Use of job descriptions

Articles 14 to 21 describe each function: its mission, its main responsibilities, the repository documents it owns, its back-up and the requirement for a COSUMAF professional card.

Each holder receives their job description on taking up the function, signs it and receives a copy; the employment contract or mandate refers to it. Annual objectives are set in accordance with PC-POL-REM-001. A job description may be supplemented by a memo from the Chief Executive Officer, without contradicting the charter.

Where a function requires a professional card, its holder does not perform it before the card is issued; the RCCI monitors expiry dates and starts the renewal three months in advance (PC-PRO-REP-001).

### Article 14 — Chairman of the Board of Directors

**Mission**: lead the work of the Board and ensure that the executive management is effectively overseen.

**Main responsibilities**:

- prepare the agenda, chair the meetings, have the minutes kept;
- receive the RCCI's alerts and hear the RCCI once a year without executive management present;
- approve the expense claims and personal expenses of the Chief Executive Officer;
- give the prior agreement provided for in Appendix 2 and follow up any overstepping of powers;
- administer the online document repository (PC-PRO-GOV-001, Article 11).

**Documents owned**: PC-COD-002.

**Back-up**: the director designated by the Board, failing that the oldest director, in accordance with the articles of association.

**Professional card**: not required.

### Article 15 — Chief Executive Officer

**Mission**: manage the Company and implement the strategy and budget set by the Board, in compliance with the licence and the regulations.

**Main responsibilities**:

- organise the Company, recruit and manage Staff Members, keep the organisation chart;
- commit the Company within the limits of Appendix 2 and keep the register of delegations;
- chair the monthly management meeting (Article 32);
- sign the filings and applications addressed to COSUMAF, BVMAC and the central securities depository;
- give the RCCI the means to perform the RCCI's mission and guarantee the RCCI's independence;
- handle operations until an operations officer is recruited (Article 22);
- report to the Board every quarter.

**Documents owned**: PC-COD-004, PC-CTR-004, PC-POL-EXE-001, PC-POL-EXT-001, PC-POL-PCA-001, PC-POL-REM-001, PC-POL-SSI-001, PC-PRO-ARC-001, PC-PRO-ORD-001, PC-PRO-REC-001, PC-PRO-SEG-001.

**Back-up**: the second senior manager.

**Professional card**: not required; the appointment is subject to the prior authorisation of COSUMAF as a senior manager.

### Article 16 — Head of Capital Markets

**Mission**: develop and conduct primary market transactions (arrangement, placement, firm underwriting) and advice to issuers.

**Main responsibilities**:

- seek mandates, prepare proposals and mandate letters;
- run transaction files through to COSUMAF approval (visa) and settlement of the securities;
- organise placement syndicates and propose allocations;
- have informed persons entered on the insider list before passing any sensitive information to them (PC-POL-CI-001);
- produce, where applicable, analysis and research, subject to the restrictions of the watch and restricted lists.

**Documents owned**: PC-CTR-001, PC-CTR-002, PC-CTR-003.

**Back-up**: the Chief Executive Officer for decisions; the Head of Sales for the day-to-day follow-up of a placement, excluding inside information.

**Professional card**: required for market activities under the General Regulation (list to be confirmed).

### Article 17 — Head of Sales

**Mission**: develop the investor client base and the distribution of products, in keeping with the interest and profile of each client.

**Main responsibilities**:

- organise prospecting and client onboarding, check that files are complete before passing them to the RCCI for high-risk clients;
- supervise the client relationship officer and validate their proposals;
- prepare marketing communications and submit them to the RCCI before release;
- monitor client satisfaction and pass any complaint to the RCCI on the same day.

**Documents owned**: PC-PRO-COM-001, PC-PRO-KYC-001.

**Back-up**: the client relationship officer for day-to-day follow-up; the Chief Executive Officer for decisions.

**Professional card**: required for solicitation of and advice to clients (to be confirmed).

### Article 18 — Head of Compliance and Internal Control (RCCI)

**Mission**: ensure that the Company complies with the regulations and its own rules, and that its risks are identified and controlled (PC-COD-003, Article 8).

**Main responsibilities**:

- advice, regulatory monitoring, prior opinions;
- annual control plan, ongoing control, follow-up of recommendations;
- risk management and risk map;
- the function of **AML/CFT officer**, unless separately appointed by the Chief Executive Officer: review of alerts and suspicious transaction reports to ANIF, which the RCCI signs alone and without referring to anyone;
- keeping the compliance registers, the document repository and the register of delegations for control purposes;
- acting as secretary of the monthly management meeting when it sits as the risk and compliance committee.

**Documents owned**: PC-COD-001, PC-COD-003, PC-POL-CI-001, PC-POL-LBC-001, PC-POL-RIS-001, PC-PRO-GOV-001, PC-PRO-OPP-001, PC-PRO-REP-001.

**Back-up**: a qualified deputy designated by the Chief Executive Officer after informing the Chairman of the Board, failing that a service provider (PC-COD-003, Article 9); the deputy AML/CFT officer is designated in advance and known to ANIF.

**Professional card**: required.

### Article 19 — Operations officer (back office)

**Mission**: ensure the safe and timely processing of orders, settlement, and clients' cash and securities.

**Main responsibilities**:

- enter and transmit orders, issue contract notes in accordance with PC-PRO-ORD-001;
- follow settlement with BVMAC, the central securities depository and the banks, and deal with fails;
- prepare payments, without the power to sign them;
- prepare the daily and monthly reconciliations of cash and securities (PC-PRO-SEG-001);
- keep the transaction files and archives (PC-PRO-ARC-001);
- report any incident to the RCCI.

**Documents owned**: none; the officer keeps the registers and logs provided for by the operations procedures.

**Back-up**: the Chief Executive Officer, with enhanced after-the-fact control by the RCCI (Article 22).

**Professional card**: to be confirmed according to the functions performed (account keeping, trading).

### Article 20 — Accountant

**Mission**: keep the Company's accounts in accordance with the revised SYSCOHADA, keep the Company's own accounts strictly separate from client accounts, and produce financial information.

**Main responsibilities**:

- record transactions and supporting documents;
- reconcile the Company's own bank accounts every month;
- prepare the monthly position, the own funds calculation and the cash flow plan (PC-POL-RIS-001);
- prepare the annual financial statements and the tax and social security returns;
- respond to the statutory auditors.

**Documents owned**: none.

**Back-up**: the external firm where the function is internal; another member of the firm where it is outsourced. The **internal owner** of the service is the Chief Executive Officer.

**Professional card**: not required.

### Article 21 — Client relationship officer

**Mission**: welcome, advise and serve clients in keeping with their profile and the Company's rules.

**Main responsibilities**:

- compile client onboarding files and update know-your-customer information;
- receive client orders through the authorised channels and pass them to operations without delay;
- present products suited to the client's profile, after a suitability test;
- report any unusual transaction to the RCCI, without informing the client;
- pass any complaint to the RCCI on the same day.

**Documents owned**: none.

**Back-up**: the Head of Sales.

**Professional card**: required for solicitation of and advice to clients (to be confirmed).

## Title V — Combinations, incompatibilities and back-up

### Article 22 — Permitted combinations

The size of the Company justifies the following combinations, within the limits allowed by the regulations, each with its compensating measure:

| Combination | Compensating measure |
| --- | --- |
| RCCI: compliance, internal control, risk management, AML/CFT and document repository | Annual independent review by a service provider; direct access to the Chairman of the Board (PC-COD-003) |
| Chief Executive Officer: operations, until an operations officer is recruited | Second signature by another signatory on every payment; reconciliations checked every month by the RCCI; quarterly surprise check; findings presented to the Chairman of the Board |
| Head of Sales: client relationship officer, where there is no separate holder | Validation by the RCCI of high-risk client onboardings; quarterly review of a sample of files |
| Head of Capital Markets: research and analysis | Watch and restricted lists; disclosure of business relationships (PC-POL-CI-001) |
| Second senior manager chosen from among the heads of department | The second senior manager does not co-sign a commitment they prepared themselves: the second signature is then given by another category A signatory, or the Chairman of the Board is informed before execution |

Any new combination is decided by the Chief Executive Officer after the written opinion of the RCCI, entered in Appendix 1 and presented to the Board at its next meeting.

### Article 23 — Incompatibilities

The same person may never:

1. be both Chairman of the Board and Chief Executive Officer;
2. hold the RCCI function together with a sales, market, operations or bank signatory function;
3. prepare a payment and sign it alone, or sign alone a payment in their own favour;
4. post entries and reconcile the same accounts alone;
5. alone change the bank details of a client or supplier and order a payment to those details;
6. approve their own expense claim, their own remuneration, their own personal transaction or their own delegation;
7. advise an issuer and, while holding inside information, recommend or execute transactions in its securities for clients;
8. hold IT administrator rights and validate transactions in the business applications.

The IT service provider and the accounting firm have no validation access to the banking or account-keeping applications.

### Article 24 — Four eyes with few people

Where there is no second person within the team, the four-eyes rule is ensured, in this order of preference:

1. by another authorised Staff Member, even from another department, who checks before execution;
2. by an after-the-fact control by the RCCI, documented in the RCCI's control plan;
3. for the acts of the Chief Executive Officer, by the second senior manager or, failing that, by the Chairman of the Board.

Each check is dated and signed, on the document or in the tool, so that it can be verified during the independent review.

### Article 25 — Back-up and absences

The table in Appendix 4 designates, for each function, a deputy and a second deputy. The deputy acts with the powers of the holder, within the same limits and subject to the same incompatibilities.

| Length of absence | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 5 business days | Automatic back-up under Appendix 4; the holder informs their deputy and their line manager before leaving |
| More than 5 business days | Memo from the Chief Executive Officer specifying the duration and the powers; information of the team and of relevant external contacts |
| More than 30 days | Entry in the register of delegations; information of the Chairman of the Board; check that no control function is performed by the person being controlled |
| Absence of a senior manager for more than 3 months, or departure | Information of COSUMAF and designation of a replacement in accordance with Article 34 |

A back-up arrangement never permits a control function to be combined with the function controlled. If no deputy meets this condition, the control task is postponed until the holder returns or entrusted to the RCCI, and the decision is recorded.

## Title VI — Delegations of authority and signature

### Article 26 — Principles

A delegation is valid if it is:

1. **in writing** and signed by the delegator and the delegate, using the template in Appendix 3;
2. **precise**: purpose, maximum amount, duration, any second signature;
3. **given to a competent person**, who has the necessary means and information;
4. **accepted** by the delegate, who knows its limits;
5. **entered in the register** in Appendix 3 before it is first used.

The delegate does not sub-delegate, unless the delegation expressly allows it. The delegator remains responsible for overseeing the use of the delegation. A delegation ends on its expiry, on its written revocation or on a change of function of the delegator or the delegate.

The Board delegates to the Chief Executive Officer within the limits of Appendix 2. The Chief Executive Officer in turn delegates, within his own limits, to the heads of department and to the second senior manager.

### Article 27 — Bank accounts and payments

1. The Board approves the list of **bank signatories**: **category A** (Chief Executive Officer, second senior manager) and **category B** (one designated head of department). The RCCI, the accountant and the operations officer are not signatories.
2. **The Company's own accounts**: a payment up to FCFA 2,000,000 is signed by one category A signatory; above that amount, it requires **two signatures**, at least one of which is category A. A payment of more than FCFA 25,000,000 not provided for in the budget also requires the prior written agreement of the Chairman of the Board.
3. **Client accounts** (segregated accounts): any outgoing transfer requires **two signatures**, whatever the amount (PC-PRO-SEG-001), and may benefit only the account holder or a market counterparty on the client's behalf.
4. Each payment is prepared by a person other than the signatories, with its supporting document. The banks' online access replicates these rules (entry, then validation by two persons).
5. The list of signatories is sent to the banks and updated on the same day as any change of function. The accountant reconciles payments against the list of signatories every month.

### Article 28 — Contractual commitments

- Contracts with clients follow the repository templates (PC-CTR-001 to PC-CTR-004); any departure from a template requires the opinion of the RCCI and, if significant, of legal counsel.
- Arrangement and placement mandates are signed by the Chief Executive Officer on the proposal of the Head of Capital Markets, after the RCCI has checked for conflicts of interest.
- Firm underwriting and standby underwriting commitments are made within the limit set by the Risk Management Policy (PC-POL-RIS-001); above that limit, the Board decides.
- Other contracts (suppliers, service providers, leases) are signed in accordance with the thresholds in Appendix 2. Material outsourcing arrangements also follow PC-POL-EXT-001.
- Sureties, endorsements and guarantees, as well as related-party agreements, are always a matter for the Board, in accordance with the OHADA Uniform Act.

### Article 29 — Regulatory filings and relations with the authorities

- Filings and applications to COSUMAF, BVMAC, the central securities depository and BEAC are prepared by the RCCI or the department concerned in accordance with PC-PRO-REP-001, and **signed by the Chief Executive Officer** or, in his absence, by the second senior manager.
- The annual internal control report is presented to the Board before it is sent (PC-COD-003, Article 24).
- Suspicious transaction reports to ANIF are made by the AML/CFT officer alone; no one may oppose them or ask for their content without need.
- Any application for prior authorisation (senior managers, shareholding, share capital, programme of activities) is approved by the Board before it is filed.

### Article 30 — Staff decisions

| Decision | Decided by | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recruitment within the approved headcount and budget | Chief Executive Officer | Signed job description; professional card obtained before performing a function that requires one |
| Recruitment outside the budget or creation of a post | Chief Executive Officer | Prior agreement of the Board |
| Appointment or departure of the RCCI | Chief Executive Officer | Prior information of the Board (PC-COD-003, Article 9) |
| Appointment or dismissal of the Chief Executive Officer, the Deputy Chief Executive Officer, the second senior manager | Board of Directors | Prior authorisation of COSUMAF |
| Disciplinary sanction, dismissal | Chief Executive Officer | Labour Code procedure; opinion of the RCCI if the breach concerns compliance |
| Remuneration and bonuses | In accordance with PC-POL-REM-001 | — |
| Leave and absences | Line manager | Deputy confirmed (Appendix 4) |

### Article 31 — Delegation matrix

The matrix in Appendix 2 summarises, for each type of decision, who prepares, who decides, who co-signs and who is informed, with the applicable thresholds. In case of doubt, the decision is referred to the higher level.

Any act taken outside a delegation is reported to the RCCI, who enters it in the register of delegations together with its regularisation.

## Title VII — Management meetings and reporting

### Article 32 — Monthly management meeting

A management meeting is held **every month**. It brings together the Chief Executive Officer, who chairs it, the second senior manager, the heads of department and the RCCI; the operations officer and the accountant are invited for the items that concern them. It may be held remotely.

It also sits as the **risk and compliance committee** provided for by the Risk Management Policy and by PC-COD-003. Its agenda includes at least:

1. business, budget and cash position;
2. dashboard of risks, incidents and limit breaches;
3. the RCCI's controls, overdue recommendations, complaints;
4. use of delegations: payments with two signatures, acts outside a delegation, back-up arrangements;
5. changes in the organisation and the regulations.

The RCCI drafts the minutes within five business days; the Chief Executive Officer signs them. Decisions and actions are recorded with a person responsible and a deadline. Minutes are kept for ten years and made available to the Board and to COSUMAF. If the meeting cannot take place, the RCCI informs the Chairman of the Board.

### Article 33 — Reporting to the Board

Every quarter, the Chief Executive Officer presents to the Board, together with the dashboard provided for by PC-COD-003:

- changes in the organisation and headcount;
- delegations granted, amended or revoked;
- commitments and payments submitted to the Chairman's agreement or close to the thresholds;
- acts taken outside a delegation and their regularisation;
- back-up arrangements lasting more than 30 days.

Each year the Board reviews the whole charter, the organisation chart and the delegation matrix.

## Title VIII — Updating and entry into force

### Article 34 — Change of function and information of COSUMAF

Any change of function (appointment, departure, combination, long-term back-up) is documented using the form in Appendix 5, opened by the Chief Executive Officer as soon as the change is decided.

- The appointment or departure of a **senior manager** is implemented only after authorisation by COSUMAF; the file is filed by the Chief Executive Officer, or by the Chairman of the Board where the change concerns the Chief Executive Officer.
- The arrival of a person performing a **function subject to a professional card** is preceded by the application for authorisation; their departure is notified to COSUMAF.
- Any change of RCCI or AML/CFT officer is notified to COSUMAF and to ANIF.
- The RCCI updates the reporting calendar in PC-PRO-REP-001, access rights, bank signatories, authorisations with BVMAC and the central securities depository, the entry in the trade and personal property register where required, and the assignment of functions in the online repository.

### Article 35 — Updating the charter

The charter is updated at each change in the organisation, and reviewed at least once a year, in accordance with PC-PRO-GOV-001. A change limited to Appendices 1, 3 and 4 (holders, deputies, register) is made by the Chief Executive Officer and presented to the Board at its next meeting; any other change is approved by the Board.

Every six months the RCCI checks that the organisation chart, the register of delegations, the list of bank signatories and the professional cards are consistent.

### Article 36 — Entry into force

This charter enters into force on the date of its adoption by the Board of Directors, as shown in the document control sheet. Each Staff Member and each director acknowledges having read it.
