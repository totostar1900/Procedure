## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This procedure sets out how the Staff Members and directors of Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company") may carry out transactions in securities for themselves or for their relatives. It implements Articles 25, 27 and 28 of the Code of Conduct (PC-COD-001) and Article 9 of the Conflicts of Interest Policy (PC-POL-CI-001).

Investing for oneself is permitted. What is not permitted: getting ahead of a client, using information obtained in the course of one's duties, or giving the appearance of having done so. The procedure protects clients and the market, and also Staff Members, who can always demonstrate that they acted properly.

### Article 2 — Persons covered

The procedure applies to all persons referred to in Article 3 of the Code of Conduct (the "Staff Members"): directors, the Chief Executive Officer, employees, interns, temporary staff, as well as service providers and agents who have access to the Company's information, to the extent provided for in their contract.

### Article 3 — Relatives and accounts covered

A personal transaction is any transaction in a financial instrument carried out:

- for the account of the Staff Member themselves;
- for the account of their spouse, their dependent children and any person living in their household;
- for the account of a legal person, a group or a tontine that they manage or control or whose investments they decide;
- for the account of any other person, where the Staff Member decides on or influences the transaction, or derives an interest from it (nominee).

The accounts of these persons are referred to below as the "covered accounts".

### Article 4 — Transactions covered

Transactions in all financial instruments are covered: shares, bonds, government securities, units in collective investment undertakings, rights and derivatives, whatever the market or country.

The following are not subject to prior authorisation, but must still be declared in the quarterly statement:

- subscriptions and redemptions of units in open-ended collective investment undertakings, unless the Company is acting as placement agent for them in an ongoing transaction;
- payments into a regular savings plan authorised once and for all, as long as its terms do not change;
- transactions carried out under a discretionary management mandate entrusted to a third party, without any instruction from or prior information to the Staff Member, provided that this mandate has been declared to the RCCI.

### Article 5 — Roles

| Function | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Staff Member | Declares their covered accounts; requests authorisation before each transaction; complies with the prohibitions; submits their statements and declarations |
| RCCI | Maintains the restricted and watch lists; reviews requests; keeps the register; checks transactions; reports |
| Chief Executive Officer | Reviews the RCCI's requests and checks the RCCI's transactions; decides in the event of disagreement |
| Desk (operations) | Executes Staff Members' orders in the same way as clients' orders, after them, upon presentation of a valid authorisation |

## Title II — Before any transaction

### Article 6 — Declaration of accounts

Each Staff Member declares to the RCCI, using Appendix 1:

- on joining, within ten days;
- every year, together with the annual declaration of interests (Appendix 3 of the Code of Conduct);
- whenever a covered account is opened or closed, within ten days,

the list of all covered securities accounts and financial instrument accounts, with the Company and elsewhere, as well as any discretionary management mandates entrusted to third parties.

### Article 7 — Where to trade

Personal transactions are carried out through the Company. The Staff Member's file in the Guichet is identified as a Staff Member account.

An account with another intermediary may only be kept or opened with the written exemption of the RCCI, in the following cases: an account that existed before the Staff Member joined, securities not accessible through the Company, an account held outside the CEMAC. The Staff Member then undertakes to provide the RCCI with a copy of each contract note and each statement, or to authorise the intermediary to send them to the RCCI directly.

### Article 8 — Restricted and watch lists

The RCCI maintains the two lists provided for in Article 9 of the Conflicts of Interest Policy, using the template in Appendix 3:

- the **restricted list**: securities of any issuer for which the Company holds an arranging, advisory or placement mandate, or inside information. No personal transaction is authorised in them, from the first contact with the issuer until the end of the stabilisation period or, failing that, until thirty days after the closing of the transaction;
- the **watch list**: issuers on which the Company holds sensitive information that does not yet justify a restriction. Personal transactions in them are refused without any reason being given.

The restricted list is communicated to Staff Members. The watch list remains **confidential**: only the RCCI and the Chief Executive Officer know its contents.

### Article 9 — Blackout periods

No personal transaction is authorised in the securities of an issuer covered by the Company during the **fifteen days** preceding the publication of its annual or half-yearly results. The RCCI maintains the calendar of these blackout periods.

## Title III — The transaction

### Article 10 — Request for authorisation

Before any transaction subject to authorisation, the Staff Member sends the RCCI the request in Appendix 2, in writing or by business e-mail. They state the security, the direction, the quantity or amount, the covered account and, in the case of a sale, the date on which the securities were purchased. They certify that they hold no inside information on the issuer and that they are not aware of any pending client order in the same security.

A request may not be submitted on behalf of a relative without naming them.

### Article 11 — Review of the request

The RCCI replies within **four business hours**. The RCCI refuses authorisation if:

- the security is on the restricted list or the watch list;
- the issuer is in a blackout period;
- a client order is pending in the same security, in the same or the opposite direction;
- a sale takes place before the end of the minimum holding period (Article 13);
- the transaction, by its size or frequency, is out of proportion to the Staff Member's situation or gives the appearance of a conflict of interest;
- the Staff Member's declarations are not up to date.

No reason need be given for a refusal. The Staff Member does not seek to find out the reason and does not discuss it with anyone: a refusal may itself reveal information.

The authorisation bears a number, is entered in the register in Appendix 4 and is valid for **forty-eight hours**. After that time, or if the order is not executed, a new request is required.

### Article 12 — Execution

The Staff Member places their order through the Guichet or by order form, stating the authorisation number. The desk checks the authorisation before any transmission. The order:

- is transmitted **after** all client orders received in the same security for the same trading session or the same auction;
- is **never aggregated** with client orders;
- in the event of partial allocation in an auction or an issue, is only allocated once clients have been fully served at the same price.

### Article 13 — Minimum holding period

A security purchased or subscribed is held for at least **thirty days** before being resold. The RCCI may authorise an early sale in exceptional and justified circumstances (unforeseen cash needs, illness, death); the exemption is reasoned and entered in the register. The rule does not apply to redemption at maturity or to the redemption of units in money market collective investment undertakings.

### Article 14 — Absolute prohibitions

Whatever authorisation has been obtained, it is prohibited:

- to trade while holding inside information, or to let a relative or a third party benefit from it;
- to trade ahead of a client, or knowing that a client is about to place an order, in the same security ("front-running");
- to trade in the opposite direction to a recommendation that the Company is about to publish or has just given to a client;
- to recommend to a third party a transaction that one is not entitled to carry out oneself;
- to take part in market manipulation, however minor, in particular in an illiquid security;
- to circumvent the procedure by having a relative or a nominee carry out the transaction.

## Title IV — After the transaction

### Article 15 — Quarterly statement

Within ten days of the end of each quarter, each Staff Member submits to the RCCI the quarterly statement in Appendix 2 of the Code of Conduct, including when they have carried out no transactions. Each transaction is shown with its authorisation number or, for an exempt transaction, a reference to the exemption.

### Article 16 — Annual declaration

Every year before 31 January, each Staff Member submits the annual declaration of interests in Appendix 3 of the Code of Conduct and updates the declaration of their accounts (Appendix 1). They certify that they have complied with this procedure.

### Article 17 — Register

The RCCI keeps the register of requests and authorisations using the template in Appendix 4: requests, replies, exemptions, executions and controls. The register is confidential.

## Title V — Control

### Article 18 — Controls by the RCCI

| Control | Frequency |
| --- | --- |
| Reconciliation of Staff Member orders in the Guichet with the authorisations in the register | Monthly |
| Verification that no Staff Member order has been transmitted ahead of a client order in the same security | Monthly |
| Reconciliation of quarterly statements with the register and, for external accounts, with the contract notes received | Quarterly |
| Follow-up of missing statements and declarations | Quarterly |
| Review of the restricted and watch lists | At each mandate and monthly |

Findings are handled in accordance with the Internal Control and Compliance Charter (PC-COD-003).

### Article 19 — Transactions of the RCCI and the Chief Executive Officer

The RCCI's requests for authorisation are reviewed by the **Chief Executive Officer**, who also checks the RCCI's statements. The Chief Executive Officer's requests are reviewed by the RCCI. The transactions of these two persons are reported each year to the Chairman of the Board of Directors and verified during the annual independent review.

### Article 20 — Breaches

A breach of this procedure constitutes a breach of the Code of Conduct, sanctioned in accordance with Article 49 thereof. In addition, an irregular transaction may have to be unwound at the Staff Member's expense, with any gain being paid over to a public-interest charity chosen by the Chief Executive Officer. Any suspicion of market abuse is handled in accordance with Article 29 of the Code of Conduct.

### Article 21 — Retention

Declarations, requests, authorisations, statements and registers are retained for **ten years**.

### Article 22 — Entry into force

This procedure enters into force on the date of its approval, as shown in the document control sheet. Each Staff Member receives a copy of it and submits their declaration of accounts (Appendix 1) within ten days.
