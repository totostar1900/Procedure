## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This procedure sets out how Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company") handles complaints from its clients: how it receives them, records them, investigates them, responds to them and learns from them.

A well-handled complaint is an opportunity: it puts a wrong right, keeps the client's trust and shows what needs to be corrected. A badly handled complaint becomes a dispute, and sometimes a matter for COSUMAF.

### Article 2 — What a complaint is

A **complaint** is an expression of dissatisfaction by a client, or by a person who has approached the Company, regarding a service that the Company has provided or should have provided, whatever its form and channel.

The following are not complaints: a request for information, a document or a statement, a request for a service to be carried out, or an opinion expressed without any grievance. They are dealt with in the normal course of the relationship.

**When in doubt, the request is treated as a complaint.** A client who writes "I am not happy" does not have to use the word "complaint" to be heard.

### Article 3 — Principles

1. **Free of charge**: making a complaint costs nothing.
2. **Accessibility**: the client may complain through any channel; they do not have to fill in a particular form.
3. **Time limits met**: acknowledgement within **two business days**, reasoned reply within **thirty days**.
4. **Independence**: the complaint is investigated by a person who is not implicated.
5. **Clarity**: the reply is in writing, in plain language, and states what the Company has found, what it has decided and why.
6. **Fairness**: when the Company is at fault, it provides redress, without waiting for the client to insist.
7. **No retaliation**: a complaint never changes the way in which the client is served.
8. **Learning**: each complaint is analysed to prevent it from recurring.

### Article 4 — Information for clients

The arrangements for complaints are set out in the securities account opening agreement (PC-CTR-004, Article 10), in the risk information notice, in the Guichet Guide and on the Company's website: channels, time limits, recourse to COSUMAF.

## Title II — Roles

### Article 5 — Who does what

| Function | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Every Staff Member | Recognises a complaint, whatever its form, and forwards it to the RCCI on the same day; never handles it alone if they are implicated |
| RCCI | Records, classifies, investigates or has investigated, drafts or approves the reply, keeps the register, analyses causes, reports |
| Chief Executive Officer | Decides on redress above the threshold in Article 13; investigates complaints implicating the RCCI; signs replies to serious complaints |
| Chairman of the Board of Directors | Investigates complaints implicating the Chief Executive Officer |
| Desk (operations) | Provides the documents relating to the transaction (signed order, time-stamp, contract note, cash ledger) and corrects any errors found |

## Title III — The steps

### Article 6 — Step 1: receipt

A complaint may arrive through:

- the **Guichet**, "Complaint" section of the client area: the client describes the facts and their request, identifies the transaction concerned and signs with a code; the Guichet generates a numbered document, files it in its Documents and sends it to the desk;
- **e-mail** to info@purposecapital.africa or to a Staff Member;
- **post** to the registered office, or **delivery by hand**;
- **telephone**, **business WhatsApp** or **appointment**.

Any complaint received through a channel other than the Guichet is forwarded to the RCCI **on the same day**. An oral complaint is put in writing by the Staff Member who received it, using the form in Appendix 1, and the client receives a copy.

### Article 7 — Step 2: recording and acknowledgement

The RCCI enters the complaint in the register in Appendix 2: number, date of receipt, client, channel, transaction concerned, subject, client's request.

The client receives an **acknowledgement within two business days**, based on the template in Appendix 3. It states the complaint number, the name of the person handling the file, the deadline for the reply and how to follow the file. In the Guichet, the acknowledgement is issued automatically and the complaint is tracked in the client's area.

If the complaint can be fully resolved within two business days, a full reply serves as the acknowledgement.

### Article 8 — Step 3: classification

The RCCI classifies the complaint:

| Severity | Examples | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | Late document, misunderstood information, question about charges | Routine investigation |
| Serious | Execution error, charges wrongly disputed, late payment, inaccurate data | Incident form (Risk Management Policy, Appendix 4); Chief Executive Officer informed |
| Major | Loss for the client, unauthorised order, suspected fraud, regulatory breach, threat to refer the matter to COSUMAF or the courts, data breach | Chief Executive Officer informed immediately; Chairman of the Board informed if a senior manager is implicated; consideration of whether to inform COSUMAF |

The RCCI also records the **category** of the complaint: account opening and maintenance, orders and execution, custody and corporate actions, cash and payments, charges, information and advice, personal data, conduct of a Staff Member, Guichet and tools, other.

If the complaint reveals a fact that may fall within AML/CFT, the RCCI examines it in accordance with the AML/CFT Policy; the RCCI never mentions a suspicion in the reply to the client.

### Article 9 — Step 4: investigation

The complaint is investigated by a person **who is not implicated**:

- as a general rule, the RCCI;
- if the RCCI is implicated, the Chief Executive Officer;
- if the Chief Executive Officer is implicated, the Chairman of the Board of Directors, who may rely on the RCCI to gather the documents.

The investigator gathers the facts: transaction documents, time-stamps, recordings, exchanges with the client, written explanations from the Staff Members concerned. The investigator compares what happened with what the agreement, the procedures and the regulations provide. The investigator may hear the client.

### Article 10 — Step 5: reply

The reply is in writing, sent through the channel chosen by the client, and placed in the client's Guichet area. It follows the template in Appendix 3 and states:

- what the Company has understood of the request;
- what it has found, with supporting documents;
- its decision: complaint upheld, partly upheld or not upheld;
- the redress proposed, where applicable, and the date on which it will be implemented;
- the reasons for any refusal, in clear terms;
- the possibility of referring the matter to COSUMAF if the client is not satisfied.

The reply is sent **within thirty days** of receipt. Where the investigation requires more time, in particular because it depends on a third party (BVMAC, central securities depository, SVT, management company, bank), the client receives, before that deadline, a holding letter explaining the reason for the delay and giving a new date, which may not be later than sixty days from receipt.

A reply to a major complaint is signed by the Chief Executive Officer.

### Article 11 — Step 6: redress

When the Company is at fault, it provides redress **without the client having to insist**:

- an execution error is corrected at no cost or loss to the client, in accordance with the Best Execution Policy; any gain belongs to the client;
- charges wrongly deducted are refunded;
- a late payment attributable to the Company may give rise to a goodwill payment calculated on the length of the delay;
- inaccurate data is rectified.

Redress is never conditional on the client waiving their other rights, except under a written settlement agreement concluded with the approval of the Chief Executive Officer.

### Article 12 — Step 7: closure

The complaint is closed when the reply is sent and, where applicable, when the redress is implemented. The register shows the closure date, the decision and the redress. If the client replies that they are not satisfied, the complaint is re-examined once by the Chief Executive Officer, or by the Chairman of the Board if the Chief Executive Officer has already ruled on it; the reply then states that it is the Company's final position.

## Title IV — Escalation and recourse

### Article 13 — Decision-making powers

| Redress | Who decides |
| --- | --- |
| Correction of an error and refund of charges wrongly deducted | RCCI, on the basis of the desk's findings |
| Goodwill payment or compensation up to FCFA 500,000 | Chief Executive Officer, on a proposal from the RCCI |
| Above FCFA 500,000, or settlement agreement | Chief Executive Officer, after informing the Chairman of the Board of Directors |

### Article 14 — Recourse to COSUMAF and the courts

The client may at any time refer the matter to COSUMAF, which receives investors' complaints about the intermediaries it licenses, or to the competent courts. The Company never dissuades the client from doing so.

When COSUMAF questions the Company about a complaint, the RCCI replies within the time limit it sets, with the complete file, after approval by the Chief Executive Officer.

### Article 15 — Complaints to be reported

The Chief Executive Officer decides, on a proposal from the RCCI, whether to inform COSUMAF when a complaint reveals a significant breach, affects several clients or may have an effect on the market. A complaint about a service provider is also handled in accordance with the Outsourcing Policy.

## Title V — Analysis and reporting

### Article 16 — Root-cause analysis

For each upheld complaint, the RCCI identifies the cause: human error, inadequate procedure, tool, service provider, insufficient information given to the client. The RCCI proposes an action to prevent it from recurring, which is entered in the recommendations follow-up table of the Internal Control and Compliance Charter.

Each quarter, the RCCI analyses all complaints using the scorecard in Appendix 4: number, categories, time limits, decisions, redress, recurring causes.

### Article 17 — Reporting

| Recipient | Frequency | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Risk and Compliance Committee | Monthly | Complaints received, in progress, overdue; major complaints |
| Board of Directors | Quarterly | Scorecard in Appendix 4; recurring causes and actions |
| COSUMAF | Annually, in the annual internal control report, and on request | Statistics and main actions |

### Article 18 — Retention

The register and the complaint files (complaint, documents, exchanges, reply, redress) are retained for **ten years** from closure.

## Title VI — Final provisions

### Article 19 — Control

The RCCI checks each quarter that time limits are met and reviews the quality of replies. Since the RCCI personally investigates most complaints, the annual independent review also examines a sample of files.

### Article 20 — Entry into force

This procedure enters into force on the date of its approval, as shown in the document control sheet. It is reviewed every year.
