These risks arise from the instruments themselves, not from the platform. They apply at Purpose Capital as anywhere else. The details are set out in the Guichet Guide, which can be consulted at any time.

| Risk | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Capital loss | A bond held to maturity repays its nominal value if the issuer pays. If sold before then, it is worth the market price on that day, which may be lower than what you paid. A share or a fund unit may lose value. |
| Interest rate | When interest rates in the zone rise, the price of a line already issued falls. The longer the line, the stronger the effect. |
| Credit | Repayment depends on the issuer's ability to pay. A Treasury of the zone carries credit risk, and a listed company carries a greater one. |
| Liquidity | A line may find no buyer at the moment you wish to exit, or only find one at a price far from the last published price. |
| Allocation | In an auction or an issue, your order may be filled in full, in part or not at all. Sums not used become available again as soon as the result is known. |
| Settlement and timing | A coupon may arrive after its due date. The ledger shows the actual date of receipt, and Purpose Capital claims from the issuer whatever is late. |
| Tax | The tax treatment depends on your situation and your country of residence. It may change, and the returns shown are stated before tax. |

**What is up to you, what is up to us.** You alone decide on your investments. Purpose Capital keeps your account, registers your securities in your name with the depository, transmits your signed orders and keeps you informed. Personalised advice, if you want it, is given through an identified adviser, after the knowledge and experience questionnaire; it is recorded and dated, and you keep a copy.

**Complaints and redress.** A complaint can be opened from your personal area or in writing to Purpose Capital's address. It receives an acknowledgement of receipt and a reference. If the response does not satisfy you, you may refer the matter to COSUMAF, which receives complaints from investors concerning the intermediaries it licenses.
