## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This policy sets out the rules that Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company") applies when it entrusts to a service provider an activity that it could carry out itself. It enables the Company to benefit from the skills and savings that a service provider offers, without losing control of that activity or of its obligations towards its clients and COSUMAF.

It details Article 21 of the Risk Management Policy (PC-POL-RIS-001).

### Article 2 — Legal basis

The policy is based on the COSUMAF General Regulation of 23 May 2023, which gives COSUMAF the power to obtain documents and to hear any person, including from third parties, without professional secrecy being enforceable against it, and which makes any change to the Company's programme of activities subject to its prior approval. It also applies Cameroonian Law No. 2024/017 of 23 December 2024 on the protection of personal data, in force since 23 June 2026.

It adopts, adapting them to the size of the Company, the IOSCO principles on outsourcing published in 2021 and the European Banking Authority guidelines on outsourcing of 2019.

### Article 3 — Definitions

- **Outsourcing**: any arrangement under which a service provider performs, on an ongoing basis, an activity, process or service that the Company would otherwise perform itself. The use of software or an online ("cloud") IT service is outsourcing where it processes or hosts data of the Company or of its clients.
- **Critical or important function**: a function whose failure or interruption would compromise the continuity of services to clients, the protection of their assets or data, the Company's compliance with regulations or its financial soundness. The classification is made using the grid in Appendix 1.
- **Service provider**: the person who performs the outsourced activity, including where it belongs to the same group as a shareholder of the Company.
- **Sub-outsourcing**: the service provider itself entrusting all or part of the activity to another service provider.
- **Contract manager**: the Staff Member designated to monitor the relationship with the service provider on a day-to-day basis.

### Article 4 — What is not outsourcing

The following fall outside this policy, and are covered by the Risk Management Policy:

- services of market infrastructures: BVMAC, the central securities depository, the settlement bank;
- services of banks, primary dealers (spécialistes en valeurs du Trésor) and account keepers with which the Company holds accounts;
- statutory or one-off engagements: statutory auditors, lawyers, notaries, advisers for a given transaction;
- purchases of supplies and services with no access to the Company's data or systems (cleaning of premises, office supplies).

## Title II — Principles

### Article 5 — The Company remains responsible

The Company remains fully responsible, towards its clients and COSUMAF, for the activities it outsources. Outsourcing does not reduce any of its obligations.

### Article 6 — What cannot be outsourced

The Company never outsources:

- effective management and strategic decisions;
- the decision to enter into a relationship with a client and the final assessment of the client's risk;
- the decision to file a suspicious transaction report with ANIF and the decision to freeze assets;
- responsibility for the compliance, risk management and internal control functions. The Company may be assisted by a service provider, in particular for the annual independent review, but the RCCI remains responsible for these functions.

The Company retains sufficient in-house expertise to understand, steer and control any outsourced activity, and to take it back in-house or transfer it to another service provider if necessary.

### Article 7 — Proportionality

The requirements of the policy are proportionate: all of them apply to the outsourcing of a critical or important function; only those marked as such in Appendices 2 and 3 apply to other outsourcing arrangements.

## Title III — Organisation

### Article 8 — Roles

| Function | Responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| Board of Directors | Approves the policy; approves any outsourcing of a critical or important function; reviews the register and the assessment of critical service providers every year |
| Chief Executive Officer | Decides on non-critical outsourcing; proposes critical outsourcing to the Board; signs contracts; designates contract managers |
| RCCI | Classifies criticality; gives a written opinion before any decision; checks contractual clauses; maintains the register; controls monitoring |
| Contract manager | Monitors service quality, incidents and indicators; prepares the annual assessment; alerts the Chief Executive Officer and the RCCI |

## Title IV — Before deciding

### Article 9 — Preliminary process

Any new outsourcing arrangement, and any renewal or material change to an existing one, follows five steps:

1. **Classification**: the RCCI classifies the activity as critical or important, or not, using the grid in Appendix 1.
2. **Risk analysis**: the project owner, together with the RCCI, analyses operational, continuity, security, data protection, legal, compliance, concentration and conflict of interest risks, using the form in Appendix 5 to the Risk Management Policy.
3. **Due diligence on the service provider**: the Company checks, using the list in Appendix 2, that the service provider has the required capacity, resources, soundness, reputation and level of security.
4. **Contract**: the contract contains the clauses in Appendix 3.
5. **Decision**: the Chief Executive Officer or, for a critical or important function, the Board of Directors decides, in the light of the RCCI's written opinion.

### Article 10 — Informing COSUMAF

Before outsourcing a critical or important function, the Company assesses whether the outsourcing changes its licensed programme of activities. If so, it seeks the prior approval of COSUMAF. In other cases, it informs COSUMAF of the outsourcing in its annual internal control report.

### Article 11 — Concentration and dependency

The Company avoids entrusting several critical functions to the same service provider without a replacement plan. For each critical function, it identifies at least one possible replacement service provider or an in-house takeover solution.

## Title V — The contract

### Article 12 — Written contract

Every outsourcing arrangement is covered by a written contract, signed before the service begins. The contract contains at least the clauses in Appendix 3, including:

- a precise description of the services and of the expected, measurable service levels;
- confidentiality and professional secrecy, including after the end of the contract;
- the protection of personal data, its location and the conditions for any transfer outside Cameroon;
- security obligations and notification of any incident to the Company within 24 hours;
- the right of access and audit of the Company, its auditors and COSUMAF, both off-site and on-site;
- a prohibition on sub-outsourcing a critical function without the Company's prior written consent;
- the service provider's business continuity plan and its testing;
- termination conditions, including termination without penalty at the request of COSUMAF;
- exit (reversibility): full return of data in a usable format and assistance with transfer to a new service provider;
- governing law and competent jurisdiction.

### Article 13 — Service providers whose terms are not negotiable

Some large online service providers offer non-negotiable standard terms. The Company may use them where those terms provide an equivalent level of protection on the points in Appendix 3, or where the gaps are offset by the Company's own measures (encryption, independent back-ups, control of administrator accounts). The gap analysis is documented and attached to the decision.

## Title VI — IT and cloud services

### Article 14 — Specific rules

For any outsourced IT service, and in particular for cloud services:

1. **Data classification**: the Company identifies the data entrusted to the service provider: client data, financial data, inside information, internal data.
2. **Location and transfers**: any hosting or processing of personal data outside Cameroon is preceded by an analysis of the level of protection in the country concerned and, where required by law, by the prior authorisation of the data protection authority.
3. **Access control**: the Company itself holds the main administrator accounts and at least one emergency account, without depending on the service provider to access its data. Multi-factor authentication is mandatory for all accounts.
4. **Encryption** of sensitive data, in transit and at rest.
5. **Logging** of the access and actions of administrators, including those of the service provider, and retention of the logs.
6. **Back-ups** independent of the main service provider for critical data, tested every quarter.
7. **Exit**: before signing, the Company knows how to recover all of its data and in what format.

## Title VII — During the contract

### Article 15 — Ongoing monitoring

The contract manager monitors service levels, incidents, changes announced by the service provider (subcontractors, data location, terms) and any information on its financial position or reputation. Any incident is reported in accordance with Article 28 of the Risk Management Policy.

### Article 16 — Annual assessment

Each service provider performing a critical or important function is assessed every year using the grid in Appendix 4. The assessment covers service quality, security, continuity, compliance with the contract and changes in risks. The results are presented to the Board of Directors together with the register.

### Article 17 — Exit strategy

For each critical or important function, the Company maintains an up-to-date written exit strategy, following the template in the second part of Appendix 4: trigger events, replacement solution, estimated duration and cost of the transfer, exit steps. It is reviewed every year and tested where reasonably practicable.

### Article 18 — End of the contract

At the end of the contract, whatever the reason, the Company ensures that all its data is returned, that it is erased by the service provider and that all access is revoked. It keeps evidence of this.

## Title VIII — Register

### Article 19 — Outsourcing register

The RCCI maintains the register of all outsourcing arrangements following the structure in Appendix 5: service provider, activity, criticality, data location, contract dates, contract manager, date of last assessment, exit strategy. The register is updated for each new contract or change, presented every year to the Board of Directors and provided to COSUMAF on request.

## Title IX — Final provisions

### Article 20 — Existing contracts

Contracts in progress at the date of adoption of this policy are entered in the register within the following month. They are brought into line with Appendix 3 at their next renewal and, for critical or important functions, within six months at most.

### Article 21 — Review and entry into force

The policy is reviewed at least once a year, and whenever there is a significant change in the regulations or in the Company's use of service providers. Any amendment is approved by the Board of Directors.

This policy enters into force on the date of its adoption by the Board of Directors, as stated in the document control sheet.
