## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This procedure sets out how Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company") learns from its mistakes: how it identifies incidents and weaknesses in its organisation, corrects them, checks that the correction works and draws lasting lessons from them.

It is the engine of the Company's continuous improvement. A well-handled incident costs once; a badly handled incident comes back. The procedure details Article 28 of the Risk Management Policy (PC-POL-RIS-001) for incidents, and Article 17 of the Internal Control and Compliance Charter (PC-COD-003) for the follow-up of recommendations. It does not replace them.

### Article 2 — Scope

The procedure applies:

- to all Staff Members, including the Chief Executive Officer, and to directors for facts of which they become aware;
- to service providers, under the conditions set out in their contract, in particular the IT service provider;
- to all incidents, whatever their cause, and to all findings from internal and external controls, whatever their origin.

### Article 3 — Definitions

- **Incident**: an event that caused, or could have caused, a loss, an error, an interruption or a breach. It may be:
  - **operational**: input or execution error, wrongly transmitted order, erroneous payment, reconciliation difference, lost document;
  - **compliance-related**: breach of regulation or of an internal procedure, late submission to COSUMAF, incomplete client file;
  - **IT or cybersecurity**: outage, intrusion, successful phishing, loss or leak of data;
  - **affecting a client**: any incident that affects a client, their assets, their orders or their data.
- **Near miss**: an error detected and stopped before it had any effect. It is reported as an incident, because it reveals the same weakness.
- **Finding**: a weakness identified by a control: ongoing control by the RCCI, annual independent review, statutory auditors, COSUMAF inspection or request, analysis of complaints, business continuity plan exercise, assessment of a service provider.
- **Root cause**: the underlying reason why the incident occurred or the finding exists; if it is removed, the problem does not recur.
- **Corrective action**: an action that removes the root cause of an incident or finding, so that it does not recur.
- **Preventive action**: an action that removes the cause of a problem that has not yet occurred, but which the analysis has revealed elsewhere.
- **Action plan**: all the actions decided for an incident or a finding; each action has an owner, a deadline and an expected evidence of completion.
- **Repeat incident**: an incident of the same nature, or with the same root cause, as an incident that occurred in the previous twelve months.

### Article 4 — Principles

1. **Everything is reported**: an incident, however small, even if avoided or corrected, is reported. What is not reported cannot be corrected.
2. **The client first**: we protect the client and correct before trying to explain.
3. **We look for a cause, not a culprit**: the analysis targets the organisation, the tools and the rules, not the person.
4. **A single register**: incidents and findings from all sources are followed up in a single register, using the same severity scale.
5. **An action is closed only on evidence**: the RCCI checks completion, then effectiveness.
6. **Every lesson changes something**: a procedure, a control, a tool or a training session.
7. **Proportionality**: the analysis effort is proportionate to severity; an isolated minor incident does not require an investigation.

### Article 5 — Relationship with other documents

This procedure organises the common workflow; the rules specific to each subject remain in their own document.

| Subject | Prevailing document |
| --- | --- |

## Title II — Roles

### Article 6 — Who does what

| Function | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Every Staff Member | Takes the immediate measures within their reach; reports the incident on the same day; takes part in the root-cause analysis; carries out the actions for which they are responsible |
| Head or manager of the department concerned | Checks the immediate measures; proposes the action plan for their area; reports on the progress of actions |
| RCCI | Records and classifies; triggers escalation; conducts or has conducted the root-cause analysis; keeps the single register; checks the completion and effectiveness of actions; reports |
| Chief Executive Officer | Decides on measures and resources for significant and major incidents; approves action plans; decides on notifications to authorities and on redress; chairs the monthly review |
| Chairman of the Board of Directors | Is informed of significant and major incidents; informs the Board; handles incidents implicating the Chief Executive Officer |
| Board of Directors | Examines the report in Appendix 5 each quarter; decides on the follow-up of major incidents where warranted |
| IT service provider | Reports without delay any incident on the systems it manages; takes part in the analysis; carries out the actions provided for in its contract |

### Article 7 — Where the RCCI or the Chief Executive Officer is implicated

No one classifies, analyses or closes an incident in which they are personally implicated.

- If the incident implicates the RCCI, or if the RCCI is responsible for an action, the Chief Executive Officer classifies the incident or checks the action.
- If the incident implicates the Chief Executive Officer, the RCCI informs the Chairman of the Board of Directors directly, who decides on the follow-up.

## Title III — Detecting and reporting

### Article 8 — Where incidents and findings come from

The single register is fed by:

- **incidents reported** by Staff Members and service providers;
- **findings from ongoing controls** by the RCCI (control sheets of the Internal Control Charter, Appendix 2);
- **recommendations of the annual independent review** and of the **statutory auditors**;
- **findings and injunctions from COSUMAF**, following an inspection, a request or a submission;
- the **quarterly analysis of complaints** (PC-PRO-REC-001, Article 16);
- **lessons learned** from the Business Continuity Plan, after a crisis or an exercise;
- **notices from BVMAC and the central securities depository** reporting an anomaly attributable to the Company.

### Article 9 — Reporting an incident

Any Staff Member who discovers an incident reports it to the RCCI **on the day it is discovered, and at the latest within 24 hours**, using the form in Appendix 1. An email covering the headings of the form is sufficient at first.

Where the incident is ongoing or appears serious (client affected, fraud, cyberattack, client assets at stake), they first alert the RCCI and the Chief Executive Officer **by telephone, immediately**, then complete the form.

Two special cases:

- an **IT security incident** is also reported immediately to the IT focal point, in accordance with Article 20 of the Information Systems Security Policy; the form of that policy may stand in for the form in Appendix 1;
- a **suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing** is not reported on the form in Appendix 1. It is reported to the AML/CFT officer in accordance with Article 23 of the AML/CFT Policy. The single register keeps no named record of it; only a failure of the framework (control not performed, alert not handled) is entered there, without mention of the suspicion.

The Staff Member reports what they know when they know it. They do not wait until they have understood everything, or until they have corrected it.

### Article 10 — A reporting culture

A Staff Member who spontaneously and in good faith reports an error they have made **is not sanctioned for that error**, in accordance with Article 30 of the Risk Management Policy. Only the following constitute misconduct within the meaning of Article 49 of the Code of Conduct:

- concealing an incident or deliberately delaying its reporting;
- destroying or altering evidence;
- intentional misconduct, fraud, or serious negligence repeated despite reminders.

The Chief Executive Officer and the RCCI set the example by reporting their own errors. A Staff Member who is reluctant to report a fact implicating a superior, or who wishes to remain anonymous, uses the whistleblowing procedure in Article 47 of the Code of Conduct.

### Article 11 — Recording findings

The RCCI enters in the single register each finding and each recommendation:

- **as soon as the control sheet is finalised**, for their own controls;
- **within five business days** of receipt of the final report, for the independent review, the statutory auditors and COSUMAF.

A report containing several recommendations gives one line per recommendation. Findings are rated in accordance with Article 16 of the Internal Control Charter; a COSUMAF finding or injunction is rated at least "significant".

## Title IV — Classifying and responding

### Article 12 — Immediate measures

Before any analysis, the person who discovers the incident and their manager:

1. **stop** whatever may make the situation worse: order suspended, payment blocked, access cut off;
2. **protect clients**: a client never bears alone the consequences of an error by the Company;
3. **preserve evidence**: orders, emails, logs, screenshots; nothing is deleted or "cleaned up";
4. **correct** what can be corrected straight away, with a second pair of eyes;
5. **note** the time and nature of each measure on the form in Appendix 1.

Where the incident interrupts an essential activity, the Chief Executive Officer assesses whether to trigger the Business Continuity Plan.

### Article 13 — Severity scale

The RCCI classifies the incident **at the latest on the business day following the report**, and immediately if it appears major. They apply the grid in Appendix 2.

| Severity | Criteria (one is enough) |
| --- | --- |
| Minor | No client affected, or error corrected the same day with no effect on the client; loss below FCFA 1,000,000; departure from an internal procedure without regulatory breach; near miss without serious potential |
| Significant | One client affected (execution error, late payment, inaccurate information); loss from FCFA 1,000,000 to less than FCFA 10,000,000; regulatory breach, for example late submission to COSUMAF or incomplete client file; interruption of a service for more than half a day; data compromised without proven disclosure; near miss that would have been major; repeated minor incident |
| Major | Loss of FCFA 10,000,000 or more, or loss bringing own funds closer to their alert threshold; loss for several clients or unexplained difference on client assets; proven fraud, internal or external; successful cyberattack or breach of client data; interruption of services to clients for more than one business day; breach liable to a COSUMAF sanction or to call the licence into question; public criticism of the Company; incident involving a senior manager; repeated significant incident |

A significant or major incident is a "significant incident" within the meaning of Article 28 of the Risk Management Policy. Where in doubt between two levels, the RCCI chooses the higher one; they may lower the rating once the facts are established, recording this in the register.

### Article 14 — Escalation

| Severity | Who is informed | Time limit | By what means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | Manager of the department concerned | On recording | Register; item at the monthly review |
| Significant | Chief Executive Officer, then Chairman of the Board of Directors | Within 24 hours of discovery | Email from the RCCI summarising the facts, the measures taken and the risks |
| Major | Chief Executive Officer and Chairman of the Board of Directors | Immediately by telephone; written note the same day | Telephone, then note from the RCCI |
| Major | Directors | Within 5 business days | Note from the Chief Executive Officer; Board meeting if the Chairman considers it necessary |

These time limits reflect Article 27 of the Risk Management Policy. Each communication is dated in the register.

### Article 15 — Notifications to authorities and third parties

For each significant or major incident, the RCCI examines whether an authority or a third party must be informed, using Appendix 4 of the Regulatory Reporting Procedure. The Chief Executive Officer decides or, if implicated, the Chairman of the Board of Directors. The RCCI prepares the notification and keeps evidence of it.

| Recipient | When | Applicable rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| COSUMAF | Significant incident affecting clients or the market: difference on client assets, fraud, cyberattack, prolonged interruption; major dispute | Without delay, written confirmation within 48 hours (PC-PRO-REP-001, Article 13) |
| ANIF | Suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing; asset freezing | By the AML/CFT officer alone, in accordance with the AML/CFT Policy; never through this workflow |
| Data protection authority and data subjects | Personal data breach | Within the legal time limit, in accordance with the Information Systems Security Policy (Article 20) |
| BVMAC, central securities depository | Settlement failure, member incident | In accordance with their rules |
| Insurer | Loss covered by the professional liability or fraud insurance | Within the time limit set in the contract |

Where in doubt, the Company informs COSUMAF: an unnecessary notification costs nothing, a missed notification may cost the licence.

### Article 16 — Affected clients

Where a client is affected, the Company informs them in writing, without waiting for a complaint, of what happened, what has been corrected and what is offered to them.

Redress follows Article 11 of the Complaints Handling Procedure, and decision-making powers follow its Article 13. Redress does not wait for the end of the root-cause analysis. If the client disputes it, their request is handled as a complaint.

## Title V — Analysing and correcting

### Article 17 — Root-cause analysis

A root-cause analysis is mandatory for:

- every significant or major incident;
- every repeat incident, even if minor;
- every finding rated major or significant.

It is conducted by the RCCI with the persons concerned, in a short meeting. It is completed, together with the action plan, **within 5 business days** for a major incident or finding, and **within 10 business days** for a significant incident or finding.

The method is the **"five whys"**: start from the observed fact and ask "why?" until reaching a cause on which the Company can act, usually after three to five questions. Each answer is based on a verified fact. The analysis stops at a cause relating to organisation, tools or rules, not at "human error": if a person made a mistake, the question is why the framework did not stop it.

The form in Appendix 4 is used. It classifies the root cause in one of the following categories: missing or inadequate procedure; missing or ineffective control; tool or system; skills or information; workload or organisation; service provider; external event.

### Article 18 — Action plan

The action plan includes corrective actions and, where the analysis shows it, preventive actions on other activities exposed to the same cause. Each action specifies:

- **what**: a concrete and verifiable action ("add a check that the account number matches before transmission", not "be more vigilant");
- **who**: an owner designated by their function;
- **when**: a deadline;
- **what evidence**: the document that will show the action has been completed (revised procedure, screenshot of the configuration, attendance list, control sheet).

Deadlines follow Article 16 of the Internal Control Charter, which also applies to incidents:

| Severity | Action plan | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major | Within 5 business days | Within 1 month |
| Significant | Within 10 business days | Within 3 months |
| Minor | If necessary | Within 6 months |

The Chief Executive Officer approves the action plan for significant and major incidents and findings, and allocates the necessary resources. For a COSUMAF finding, the deadline it sets prevails if it is shorter.

### Article 19 — Follow-up and extension of deadlines

The owner of each action reports to the RCCI at the deadline, or earlier if they expect a delay.

A deadline may be extended only once, by a reasoned decision of the Chief Executive Officer recorded in the register. The extension of an action linked to a major incident or finding, or to a COSUMAF finding, is brought to the attention of the Chairman of the Board of Directors.

Overdue actions are examined at each monthly review; those more than three months overdue are presented to the Board of Directors, in accordance with Article 17 of the Internal Control Charter.

### Article 20 — Closure and verification of effectiveness

An action is **completed** when its owner has provided the expected evidence and the RCCI has checked it. The RCCI dates and signs off the closure in the register.

For significant and major incidents and findings, the RCCI also checks the **effectiveness** of the action, about three months after its completion: test on a sample of transactions, control included in the annual control plan, or confirmation that the incident has not recurred. If the action is not effective, the line is reopened and a new analysis is carried out.

An incident or finding is closed only when all its actions are completed and, where applicable, found effective.

## Title VI — Register and reporting

### Article 21 — Single register

The RCCI keeps the register of incidents and action plans using the template in Appendix 3. This single register combines the incident register provided for by the Risk Management Policy and the recommendations follow-up table provided for by the Internal Control Charter.

- Each incident receives a number INC-year-number; each finding a number CST-year-number; each action a letter (INC-2027-004-a, -b…).
- The register is kept electronically; the RCCI writes to it, the Chief Executive Officer and the members of the risk and compliance committee consult it.
- Reporting forms, analysis forms and evidence of completion are filed in a folder per number.
- The register and the files are kept for **ten years** after closure, and made available to COSUMAF on request.

### Article 22 — Monthly review

The risk and compliance committee, the monthly management meeting provided for by the Risk Management Policy and the Internal Control Charter, devotes an item on its agenda to the register. The RCCI presents:

- new incidents and findings of the month, with their severity;
- the status of action plans: open, completed, overdue;
- actions to be closed and effectiveness checks;
- repeat incidents and lessons to be shared.

Decisions (approval of a plan, extension of a deadline, resources allocated) are recorded in the minutes of the meeting and carried over into the register.

### Article 23 — Quarterly report to the Board of Directors

Each quarter, the RCCI prepares the report in Appendix 5, approved by the Chief Executive Officer. It is included in the quarterly dashboard presented to the Board of Directors (Internal Control Charter, Article 24; Risk Management Policy, Article 29). The Board may ask for explanations on any incident or any overdue action; its decisions are recorded in the minutes of the meeting.

### Article 24 — Annual summary

Each year, the RCCI summarises the register:

- in the **annual internal control report**, submitted to COSUMAF before 31 March, under the headings provided for by the outline in Appendix 4 of the Internal Control Charter: findings and measures taken, incidents that affected clients, IT incidents, external controls and follow-up of recommendations;
- in the **update of the risk map** (Risk Management Policy, Article 9): each risk linked to a significant or major incident of the year is re-rated, and a risk revealed by an incident but missing from the risk map is added to it. A major incident triggers an update of the risk map without waiting for the annual review.

## Title VII — Learning lessons

### Article 25 — Updating documents

Where the analysis shows that a rule is missing, unclear or inadequate, the action plan provides for the amendment of the document concerned. The RCCI records the request in accordance with Article 8 of the Document Control Procedure (PC-PRO-GOV-001), with the number of the incident or finding, and the deadline of the action applies to the publication of the revised document.

### Article 26 — Training and sharing

Where the cause lies in a lack of skills or information, the action plan provides for training or awareness-raising, included in the training plan in accordance with the Training Policy (PC-POL-FOR-001). The evidence of completion is the attendance list or the acknowledgement of reading.

Lessons useful to everyone are shared without naming anyone: through an item at the monthly review, through a short message to the Staff Members concerned, and each year during the mandatory training provided for by the Internal Control Charter (Article 21).

### Article 27 — Indicators

The RCCI monitors the following indicators, presented each quarter in the report in Appendix 5.

| Indicator | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Incidents reported, by severity | No target: a high number of reported minor incidents is a sign of a good reporting culture |
| Incidents reported within 24 hours | 100% |
| Root-cause analyses completed on time | 100% |
| Open actions | Trend monitoring |
| Overdue actions | 0 |
| Actions more than three months overdue | 0 |
| Repeat incidents | 0 |
| Actions found effective at verification | At least 90% |
| Incidents discovered by a third party (client, COSUMAF, BVMAC) before being reported internally | 0 |

## Title VIII — Final provisions

### Article 28 — Control

Each quarter, the RCCI checks that incidents known from other sources (complaints, reconciliations, IT logs, letters from authorities) are indeed in the register.

As the RCCI keeps the register and checks the actions themselves, the annual independent review (Internal Control Charter, Article 18) examines a sample of incidents and closed actions, and compliance with the time limits of this procedure.

### Article 29 — Entry into force

This procedure enters into force on the date of its approval, as shown on the document control sheet. It is reviewed every year.
