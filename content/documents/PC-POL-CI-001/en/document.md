## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This policy sets out how Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company") identifies, prevents, manages and discloses conflicts of interest that may arise in the course of its activities. It details and supplements Title IV of the Code of Conduct (PC-COD-001), which lays down the underlying principles.

Its aim is that no client is harmed by an interest of the Company, of a Staff Member, of a shareholder or of another client.

### Article 2 — Scope

The policy applies:

- to all of the Company's activities: reception, transmission and execution of orders, dealing, advice to issuers and arranging, placement of securities, distribution of units in collective investment schemes, account keeping and custody, investment advice;
- to all relevant persons within the meaning of Article 3;
- to the Company's relationships with its clients, issuers, counterparties, service providers and shareholders.

### Article 3 — Definitions

- **Conflict of interest**: a situation in which the interest of the Company, of a relevant person or of a client is liable to conflict with the interest of another client, to the detriment of the latter. The conflict may be potential or actual.
- **Relevant person**: any director, senior manager, employee, intern or service provider of the Company, and any person closely associated with them (spouse, dependent child, intermediary acting on their behalf, company they control).
- **Sensitive information**: any non-public information relating to a client, an issuer, a transaction or an order, the circulation of which could benefit one party to the detriment of another.
- **Chinese wall**: all the organisational, physical and IT measures that prevent sensitive information from flowing between separated activities.

### Article 4 — Principles

1. The client's interest takes precedence over that of the Company and of relevant persons.
2. Prevention takes precedence over management: the Company organises its activities so that conflicts do not arise.
3. Disclosure to the client is a last resort. It never replaces prevention and management measures.
4. A conflict that cannot be managed in a way that protects the client leads the Company to decline the transaction.

## Title II — Identification of conflicts

### Article 5 — Conflicts map

The RCCI maintains the map of potential conflicts of interest specific to the Company's activities (Appendix 1). For each situation, it states the interests involved, the risk to the client, the prevention measures and the person responsible for applying them.

The map is reviewed at least once a year, and whenever a new activity, a new product or a significant transaction (initial public offering, bond issue, arranging mandate) is launched.

### Article 6 — Situations to be identified

A relevant person is in a conflict of interest situation in particular where the Company or that person:

- is likely to make a gain or avoid a loss at the expense of a client;
- has an interest in the outcome of a service or transaction that is distinct from the client's interest;
- has an incentive to favour one client or group of clients over another;
- carries on the same business as the client, for its own account;
- receives from a third party, in connection with the service provided to the client, a benefit other than the commission normally charged.

### Article 7 — Declaration by relevant persons

Each relevant person declares:

- on recruitment, and every year thereafter, their personal interests by means of the annual declaration of interests (Appendix 3 to the Code of Conduct);
- without delay, and at the latest within 48 hours, any conflict situation they identify in the performance of their duties, by means of the declaration form (Appendix 2 to this policy).

In case of doubt, the relevant person makes a declaration: the RCCI assesses it. Failure to declare constitutes misconduct within the meaning of Article 49 of the Code of Conduct.

## Title III — Prevention

### Article 8 — Segregation of duties and Chinese wall

The following activities are separated: (i) advice to issuers, arranging and placement of transactions; (ii) reception, transmission and execution of client orders; (iii) research and financial analysis; (iv) management of the Company's own accounts.

The separation is based on:

- IT access rights granted on a need-to-know basis and reviewed every six months;
- separate files and folders for each transaction, with named-user access;
- internal meetings and exchanges organised so as not to expose sensitive information to unauthorised persons;
- an authorisation process for any crossing of the wall: a person who must be involved in a sensitive transaction is first entered by the RCCI on the insider list and informed of their obligations.

### Article 9 — Watch list and restricted list

The RCCI maintains:

- a confidential **watch list** of issuers on which the Company holds sensitive information; transactions in these securities are subject to after-the-event control;
- a **restricted list** of securities in which relevant persons may not deal for their own account, and on which the Company publishes no recommendation or analysis for the duration of an arranging or placement mandate.

### Article 10 — Measures specific to the size of the Company

The size of the Company means that certain functions are combined, within the limits allowed by the regulations where the size of the firm justifies it. For as long as these combinations exist, the following compensating measures apply.

| Combination | Risk | Compensating measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compliance and internal audit performed by the same function (RCCI) | The RCCI controls its own arrangements | Annual independent review of the compliance framework by an external service provider, with a report presented to the Board of Directors |
| Operations function performed by the Chief Executive Officer | The same person decides and executes; they may make choices that favour the Company | Dual approval of transactions outside the standard procedure; monthly after-the-event control by the RCCI of reconciliations and allocations; quarterly report to the Board |
| IT outsourced to an external service provider | Service provider's access to client data and sensitive information | Confidentiality and conflicts of interest clause in the contract; named and logged accounts; annual access review by the RCCI |
| Small headcount in the sales and markets functions | The same Staff Member advises the issuer and places with investors | Written disclosure to investors of the Company's role in the transaction; allocations approved by the RCCI |

### Article 11 — Remuneration and benefits

The remuneration of Staff Members does not create incentives contrary to clients' interests. The variable component is not based solely on the volume of commissions or transactions, and takes account of compliance with the rules.

Any commission or retrocession received from a third party in connection with a service provided to a client, in particular from a management company for the distribution of units in collective investment schemes, must enhance the quality of the service provided, be disclosed to the client before the service is provided, and not prevent the Company from acting in the client's best interests. In the distribution of collective investment schemes, the Company does not exclude any product provider and offers products solely on the basis of their suitability for the client's profile.

Gifts and benefits received by Staff Members are governed by Article 22 of the Code of Conduct.

### Article 12 — Directors, senior managers and shareholders

The directors and the Chief Executive Officer declare their appointments and interests under the conditions of Article 7.

Any agreement between the Company and one of its directors, its Chief Executive Officer, a significant shareholder or a company in which any of them has an interest is subject to the related-party agreements procedure of the OHADA Uniform Act on Commercial Companies and Economic Interest Groups. The interested director takes no part in the deliberation or the vote.

Where the Company intends to act as arranger, placing agent or adviser to an issuer in which a director, a senior manager or a shareholder holds interests, the matter is referred to the RCCI before the mandate is accepted. The decision is taken by the Board of Directors or, where one exists, by its audit and risk committee, with the interested person abstaining.

### Article 13 — Order handling and allocation

Client orders are handled in the chronological order in which they are received and are never deferred in favour of a transaction for the Company's own account or for a personal account.

In the event of oversubscription of an issue placed by the Company, the allocation rules are set in advance in the information memorandum and applied identically to all subscribers. The accounts of the Company, its Staff Members and its shareholders are served only after clients, and only if the issuer has expressly agreed.

## Title IV — Management of conflicts

### Article 14 — Decision-making process

1. **Reporting**: the relevant person completes the form in Appendix 2 and sends it to the RCCI.
2. **Analysis**: the RCCI classifies the situation within 2 business days: no conflict, conflict controlled by existing measures, or conflict requiring a decision.
3. **Decision**: depending on the case, the RCCI prescribes additional measures, the Chief Executive Officer decides whether the transaction proceeds, or the Board of Directors rules where the conflict involves the Chief Executive Officer, a director or a shareholder.
4. **Disclosure to the client** if the conflict persists after the measures taken (Article 15).
5. **Recording** in the conflicts register (Article 16).

Where the conflict involves the RCCI personally, the report is sent to the Chief Executive Officer, who informs the Chairman of the Board of Directors.

### Article 15 — Disclosure to the client and declining the service

Where the measures taken are not sufficient to ensure, with reasonable confidence, that the risk of damage to the client's interests will be prevented, the Company informs the client in writing, before providing the service, of the nature and source of the conflict and the measures taken to mitigate its effects (template in Appendix 3). The client may then decline the service.

The disclosure is sufficiently detailed to enable the client to take an informed decision. A general statement is not sufficient.

If the conflict cannot be managed in a way that protects the client, even once informed, the Company declines to provide the service or carry out the transaction.

## Title V — Monitoring and control

### Article 16 — Conflicts of interest register

The RCCI maintains a register of conflicts of interest that have arisen or may arise (structure in Appendix 4). Each entry states the date, the activity and the persons involved, the nature of the conflict, the measures taken, the decision and, where applicable, the disclosure made to the client.

The register and the related documents are kept for at least ten years. They are provided to COSUMAF on request.

### Article 17 — Control and reporting

The RCCI includes the conflicts of interest management framework in its annual control plan. Every year it presents to the Board of Directors a report covering the number and nature of conflicts recorded, the decisions taken, the cases of client disclosure and of declining the service, and the updates to the conflicts map.

### Article 18 — Training

This policy forms part of the mandatory training provided for in Article 46 of the Code of Conduct. Each Staff Member acknowledges having read it.

## Title VI — Final provisions

### Article 19 — Informing clients about the policy

A summary of this policy is provided to clients at client onboarding and made available to them on request. It is published on the Company's website.

### Article 20 — Sanctions

Any breach of this policy exposes the person responsible to the sanctions provided for in Article 49 of the Code of Conduct, without prejudice to sanctions imposed by COSUMAF.

### Article 21 — Review and entry into force

The policy is reviewed at least once a year by the RCCI and whenever there is a significant change in the activities or the regulations. Any amendment is approved by the Board of Directors.

This policy enters into force on the date of its adoption by the Board of Directors, as stated in the document control sheet.
