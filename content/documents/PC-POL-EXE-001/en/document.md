## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This policy describes the measures that Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company") takes to obtain, when it receives, transmits or executes its clients' orders, the best possible result taking into account the nature of the order and the characteristics of the market concerned. It details Article 14 of the Code of Conduct (PC-COD-001).

### Article 2 — Scope

The policy applies to all orders received from clients, whatever the channel through which they are received, relating to:

- securities listed on the BVMAC: shares, bonds and other securities traded on the electronic trading platform;
- government securities issued by CEMAC States (Treasury bills and bonds), on the primary market by auction and on the secondary market;
- subscriptions to primary issues placed by public offering;
- subscriptions and redemptions of units or shares in collective investment schemes.

It applies to all clients. The arrangements specific to non-professional clients and institutional investors are set out in Article 5.

### Article 3 — Definitions

- **Best execution**: the obligation to take all sufficient steps to obtain the best possible result for the client, having regard to the factors in Article 4. It is an obligation of means (best endeavours), assessed across all orders and not order by order.
- **Execution venue**: a market, platform or counterparty with which an order is executed (Appendix 1).
- **Total cost**: the price of the instrument plus all costs borne by the client, including the Company's commissions and market, settlement and custody fees.
- **Specific instruction**: an express instruction from the client concerning the price, timing, venue or terms of execution of their order.

## Title II — Execution factors and criteria

### Article 4 — Best execution factors

The Company takes into account the following factors: the price of the instrument; the total cost; the speed of transmission and execution; the likelihood of execution and settlement; the size of the order relative to the liquidity of the security; the nature of the order and any other consideration relevant to its execution.

### Article 5 — Relative importance of the factors

| Client category | Decisive factor | Other factors taken into account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Non-professional client (individual, small business, association) | Total cost | Likelihood of execution, then speed |
| Institutional investor (bank, insurer, pension fund, CIS) | Price and likelihood of execution | Order size and market impact, cost, speed |
| Order of significant size relative to the liquidity of the security | Price impact and likelihood of execution | Splitting over several sessions, cost |

An order is deemed to be of significant size where it exceeds 20% of the average daily volume traded in the security over the last three months, or where its execution in a single session is likely to move the price beyond the applicable price fluctuation limit.

### Article 6 — Specific client instructions

Where the client gives a specific instruction, the Company executes the order in accordance with it. The Company is then deemed to have satisfied its best execution obligation for the part of the order covered by the instruction.

The Company does not encourage a client to give a specific instruction where it knows or ought to know that the instruction will prevent it from obtaining the best result. Where an instruction appears to be unfavourable to the client (for example, a market order on an illiquid security), the relationship manager warns the client before execution and keeps a record of that warning.

## Title III — Execution by instrument category

### Article 7 — Securities listed on the BVMAC

The BVMAC is the sole execution venue for orders in securities listed on it. Trading takes place by daily fixing, Monday to Friday: orders are matched in a single auction and executed at a single price. Best execution therefore concerns the quality of order handling rather than the choice of venue. The Company:

1. transmits orders received before the session cut-off time (Appendix 1) to that day's session, and orders received after that time to the next session, in the chronological order in which they were received;
2. advises the client on the appropriate order type: a limit order is recommended by default, given the often limited liquidity of securities; a market order is accepted only after the client has been informed of the risk of execution at a price far from the last price;
3. proposes, for an order of significant size, that it be split over several sessions, with the client's agreement;
4. informs the client of the price fluctuation limits, the period of validity of the order and the consequences of a reservation or a freeze of the security;
5. checks, before transmission, that sufficient securities or cash are available and that the order is consistent (direction, quantity, limit); a manifestly erroneous order is confirmed with the client before transmission.

### Article 8 — Subscriptions to primary issues

For issues by public offering and for auctions, the client's order is a subscription on the terms set by the issuer or by the auctioning authority. The Company transmits each subscription within the subscription period, without delaying it or altering its content, and applies the published allocation rules. Where the Company is itself arranger or placing agent for the issue, the rules of the Conflicts of Interest Management Policy (PC-POL-CI-001, Article 13) apply.

### Article 9 — Government securities on the secondary market

Government securities are traded over the counter, mainly between primary dealers (spécialistes en valeurs du Trésor); transactions are reported to the unlisted (hors cote) segment of the BVMAC under the conditions laid down in its General Regulation. For each client order, the Company:

1. requests quotes from at least two primary dealers among those listed in Appendix 1, or three for an order of significant size;
2. compares the quotes obtained in terms of yield to maturity and clean price, costs included;
3. selects the best executable quote, unless the client has given a specific instruction;
4. records the quotes received, the time and the reason for the choice on the quote comparison sheet (Appendix 4).

Where only one quote can be obtained, the Company informs the client before execution and keeps a record of this.

### Article 10 — Collective investment schemes

Subscriptions and redemptions of units in collective investment schemes are executed at the applicable net asset value, calculated by the management company. The Company transmits the order before the centralisation cut-off time set in the scheme's prospectus, so that it is executed at the first possible net asset value.

## Title IV — Order handling

### Article 11 — Receipt and time-stamping

Every order is time-stamped on receipt, whatever the channel: signed order form at the counter, recorded telephone line, business e-mail or electronic platform. Orders received through an unrecorded channel are not accepted (Code of Conduct, Article 33). The order states at least the security, the direction, the quantity, the type and, where applicable, the limit and the period of validity.

### Article 12 — Order of handling, aggregation and allocation

Orders are transmitted in the chronological order in which they are received. They are never deferred in favour of an order for the account of the Company or of a Staff Member.

Orders from several clients in the same security and in the same direction may be aggregated where aggregation does not disadvantage any of them. In the event of partial execution, the securities are allocated pro rata to the quantities requested, with odd lots allocated in the chronological order of receipt. An order for the Company's own account or for a Staff Member is never aggregated with a client order.

### Article 13 — Confirmation and reporting

The client receives a trade confirmation no later than the business day following execution. The confirmation states the date and time of execution, the venue, the security, the direction, the quantity, the price and a breakdown of the costs. An order not executed by the end of its period of validity is reported to the client.

### Article 14 — Execution errors

Any error attributable to the Company (wrong direction, quantity, security or account) is corrected as soon as it is detected, at no cost or loss to the client. The error is reported to the RCCI, recorded in an incident register and analysed to prevent it from recurring. Any gain resulting from an error does not accrue to the Company: it is allocated to the client concerned.

## Title V — Control and review

### Article 15 — Control of execution quality

Every quarter the RCCI controls execution quality using the grid in Appendix 3: transmission times, compliance with chronological order, execution rate, comparison of prices obtained with session prices, competitive quoting for government securities, errors and complaints. Discrepancies are analysed and give rise to corrective measures.

### Article 16 — Review and reporting

The policy and the list of execution venues are reviewed at least once a year, and whenever there is a significant change: new BVMAC trading rules, entry into operation of a government securities trading platform, a new activity. Every year the Chief Executive Officer presents to the Board of Directors a report on execution quality based on the quarterly controls.

### Article 17 — Information to clients

A summary of this policy (Appendix 2) is provided to the client at client onboarding, before the first order. The client is informed of any material change. On request, the Company demonstrates to the client that their orders have been executed in accordance with the policy.

### Article 18 — Retention

Orders, telephone and electronic recordings, quotes obtained, trade confirmations and control grids are kept for at least ten years.

## Title VI — Final provisions

### Article 19 — Sanctions

Any breach of this policy exposes the person responsible to the sanctions provided for in Article 49 of the Code of Conduct, without prejudice to sanctions imposed by COSUMAF.

### Article 20 — Entry into force

This policy enters into force on the date of its adoption by the Board of Directors, as stated in the document control sheet.
