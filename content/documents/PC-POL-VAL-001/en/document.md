## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This policy states which transactions must be approved before Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company") commits itself, by whom, and with what written evidence.

Its aim is simple: that no commitment exposing the Company, its own funds, its clients or its reputation is made by a single person, without review by a second person, and without the opinion of the compliance function where the stakes warrant it.

The Company has few Staff Members. The policy therefore organises a realistic four-eyes review: between two Staff Members for routine transactions, between the Chief Executive Officer and the RCCI for significant commitments, and with the Chairman of the Board of Directors or the Board itself above certain thresholds.

### Article 2 — Scope

The policy applies to all Staff Members and senior managers of the Company, for:

- commitments made on the capital markets: arranging and placement mandates, firm underwriting and standby underwriting commitments, participation in a syndicate;
- onboarding of clients, issuers or counterparties presenting a high risk;
- the launch of new products, new activities, new markets and significant outsourcing;
- client orders falling outside the routine framework;
- the investment of the Company's own funds;
- the Company's payments and financial commitments, as regards principles only.

It does not replace the operational procedures: it sets the approval level and refers to those procedures for how transactions are carried out.

### Article 3 — Definitions

- **Commitment**: any act that binds the Company towards a third party or exposes its own funds: signature of a contract or mandate letter, firm offer, acceptance of a syndicate share, payment, investment, guarantee.
- **Approval**: written consent, prior to the commitment, given by the competent person or body in accordance with Appendix 1. An archived business email or an electronic sign-off counts as writing; oral consent does not count as approval.
- **Four-eyes review**: the transaction is prepared by one person and checked or approved by another, who is not under the first person's authority for that transaction.
- **RCCI opinion**: written opinion of the Head of Compliance and Internal Control on regulatory compliance, anti-money laundering, conflicts of interest and the risks of the transaction.
- **Reference own funds**: the amount of the Company's own funds derived from the latest monthly calculation provided for by the Risk Management Policy (PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 13).
- **Commitment committee**: the meeting provided for in Article 10.

## Title II — Principles and approval levels

### Article 5 — Principles

1. **No commitment without prior approval at the right level.** An approval given after the commitment is not an approval: it is an incident, reported to the RCCI.
2. **Everything is in writing.** The request, the RCCI opinion and the decision are recorded and retained (Title VII).
3. **The RCCI gives an opinion before any significant commitment.** The RCCI rules on compliance, anti-money laundering and conflicts of interest. The RCCI does not decide in place of management, but the RCCI's opinion cannot be ignored (Article 7).
4. **Whoever proposes does not approve alone.** The person sponsoring the transaction can never be the only one to approve it.
5. **Escalation rather than improvisation.** Where there is doubt about the competent level, the higher level applies.
6. **Refusal is always possible.** Anyone called upon to approve may refuse or ask for further information. No commercial objective justifies overriding this; a refusal made in good faith exposes its author to no adverse measure.

### Article 6 — Approval levels

| Level | Who approves | For what, as a general rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N1 — Operational four-eyes review | One Staff Member prepares, a second checks and approves | Routine transactions within the procedures |
| N2 — Chief Executive Officer after RCCI opinion | Chief Executive Officer, in light of the written opinion of the RCCI, in the commitment committee where applicable | Significant commitments within the limits approved by the Board |
| N3 — Prior consent of the Chairman of the Board | Chairman of the Board of Directors, on written request of the Chief Executive Officer accompanied by the RCCI opinion | Commitments approaching the limits, or where the Chief Executive Officer has a conflict of interest |
| N4 — Board of Directors | Board of Directors, by resolution | Commitments exceeding the limits or the powers of the Chief Executive Officer, or modifying the business plan |

Appendix 1 shows, for each type of transaction, the required level and the thresholds. Thresholds expressed as a percentage of own funds are assessed against the reference own funds and, for firm underwriting and guarantees, against the total outstanding commitments.

The Chairman of the Board does not manage the Company: the Chairman's consent at level N3 is a control, not a management decision. The Chairman reports on it to the Board at its next meeting.

### Article 7 — RCCI opinion

The RCCI gives a written opinion within **2 business days** of receiving a complete file, using the commitment request (Appendix 2). The opinion is: favourable, favourable subject to conditions, or unfavourable.

Where the Chief Executive Officer decides to commit despite an unfavourable opinion or without meeting the conditions set, the Chief Executive Officer gives written reasons for the decision and informs the Chairman of the Board of Directors within **2 business days**. For firm underwriting or a standby underwriting commitment, an unfavourable RCCI opinion raises the decision to level N4.

The RCCI does not approve a transaction that the RCCI sponsors. Nor may the RCCI be the second reviewer of a transaction that the RCCI will later control; in that case, the second review is given by another authorised Staff Member.

### Article 8 — Conflict of interest, absence and deputising

A person who has a personal interest in the transaction does not approve it. That person declares it in accordance with the Conflicts of Interest Management Policy (PC-POL-CI-001, Article 7).

| Situation | Who approves instead |
| --- | --- |
| The Chief Executive Officer has a conflict of interest, or sponsors the transaction alone | Chairman of the Board of Directors (N3), or the Board if the N4 threshold is reached |
| A director or shareholder has an interest in the transaction | Board of Directors, the person concerned abstaining (PC-POL-CI-001, Article 12) |
| The Chief Executive Officer is absent | Deputy Chief Executive Officer if one has been appointed; failing that, the decision awaits the Chief Executive Officer's return, except in an emergency (Article 9) |
| The RCCI is absent or has a conflict | The Chief Executive Officer seeks the opinion of the independent review provider or, failing that, the prior consent of the Chairman of the Board |

No delegation is granted to a Staff Member to approve a transaction that the Staff Member has prepared.

### Article 9 — Emergency

An emergency removes no approval level; it speeds up the workflow:

- the RCCI opinion and the consent of the Chairman of the Board may be given by archived business email, within one business hour where the transaction so requires;
- the Board may meet by videoconference under the conditions of its rules of procedure (PC-COD-002, Article 16) or, if the articles of association so permit, be consulted in writing;
- a transaction that cannot be approved in time is not carried out.

## Title III — Capital markets commitments

### Article 10 — Commitment committee

The commitment committee examines the commitments in Articles 11 to 13 before a decision is taken. It brings together:

- the **Chief Executive Officer**, who chairs it and decides;
- the **Head of Capital Markets**, who presents the file;
- the **RCCI**, who gives an opinion and does not take part in the decision.

The Head of Sales is invited where the capacity to place securities with clients is at stake. The committee may be held alongside the monthly risk committee (PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 8), by videoconference, or by exchange of archived emails where the file is simple. It deliberates validly only in the presence of the Chief Executive Officer and the RCCI, or in light of the latter's written opinion.

Each meeting is recorded in **minutes** (Appendix 3), signed by the Chief Executive Officer and the RCCI within **2 business days**, stating the file, the RCCI opinion, the decision, its conditions and, where applicable, the approval level then required.

While the position of Head of Capital Markets remains vacant, the sponsor of the file presents it; if that sponsor is the Chief Executive Officer, the decision falls to level N3.

### Article 11 — Arranging and placement mandates

A best-efforts arranging or placement mandate (PC-CTR-001) does not commit the Company's own funds, but it engages its liability and reputation towards investors and COSUMAF.

Before signing any mandate letter or agreement:

1. the Head of Capital Markets compiles the file: presentation of the issuer and the transaction, timetable, remuneration, identified risks;
2. the desk verifies the identity of the issuer, its senior managers and its beneficial owners in accordance with PC-PRO-KYC-001;
3. the RCCI checks conflicts of interest (PC-POL-CI-001, Articles 8, 9 and 12), places the issuer on the watch list and opens the insider list;
4. the commitment committee examines the file and the Chief Executive Officer decides (N2).

The decision falls to the Board of Directors where the issuer is linked to a director, a senior manager or a shareholder of the Company (PC-POL-CI-001, Article 12), or where the issuer or the transaction presents a high risk within the meaning of the AML/CFT Policy.

The signed agreement complies with template PC-CTR-001. Any departure from the template is subject to the prior opinion of the RCCI (PC-COD-003, Article 20).

### Article 12 — Firm underwriting and standby underwriting commitment

Firm underwriting or a standby underwriting commitment (PC-CTR-002) commits the Company's own funds: if the securities are not placed, the Company buys them. It is the heaviest commitment the Company can make.

**Prerequisites, whatever the amount:**

- a scenario of total non-placement is quantified: possible loss on the securities retained, effect on own funds and on cash;
- after this scenario, own funds remain above the alert threshold of the Risk Management Policy (PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 13), and available cash, once the commitment is paid, still covers at least six months of fixed costs (Article 14 of that policy), unless a bank financing line has been confirmed in writing;
- the commitment complies with the prudential standards set by COSUMAF;
- the information memorandum names the Company as guarantor;
- the Company is able to hold the securities in an own account separate from client accounts (PC-CTR-002, Article 8).

**Approval levels** (total outstanding firm underwriting and guarantee commitments, as a percentage of reference own funds):

| Outstanding after the transaction | Level |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 15% | N2 — Chief Executive Officer after the commitment committee and the RCCI opinion |
| More than 15% and up to 25% | N3 — in addition, prior written consent of the Chairman of the Board of Directors |
| More than 25%, or any firm underwriting of shares | N4 — prior resolution of the Board of Directors |

The statement of the Board's consent provided for in Article 7 of PC-CTR-002 is completed only on the basis of the decision obtained at the right level. The Head of Capital Markets monitors the commitment until it is settled (Article 14).

### Article 13 — Participation in a syndicate

Where the Company is **lead manager** of a placement syndicate, the transaction follows Article 11, and Article 12 for any share of the guarantee.

Where the Company is a **member** of a syndicate led by another intermediary (PC-CTR-003):

- without a guarantee commitment, the Head of Capital Markets proposes and the Chief Executive Officer decides after the RCCI opinion, which covers the lead manager, the issuer, selling restrictions and conflicts of interest; the opinion may be given by email without a committee meeting;
- with a share of the guarantee, Article 12 applies to that share; as the members' commitments are several and not joint, only the Company's share is taken into account, unless otherwise stipulated, in which case the transaction is raised to level N4.

### Article 14 — Monitoring of commitments until settlement

The Head of Capital Markets keeps up to date, for each outstanding commitment, the status of subscriptions and the amount guaranteed. The Head of Capital Markets alerts the Chief Executive Officer and the RCCI without delay when the placement suggests that the guarantee will be called.

Securities retained after a guarantee is called do not constitute a trading position. The commitment committee adopts a disposal plan within one month, presented to the Board at its next meeting. Client orders are always served before any disposal for own account (PC-CTR-002, Article 8).

## Title IV — Clients, counterparties, products and activities

### Article 15 — High-risk onboarding

The rules for deciding on client onboarding are set by the Client Onboarding Procedure (PC-PRO-KYC-001, Article 12) and the AML/CFT Policy (PC-POL-LBC-001, Articles 10, 12, 14 and 15). They are not repeated here. In summary:

- low or standard risk: decision at level N1, controlled by the RCCI within one month;
- high risk, PEP, complex structure (layered companies, trust, beneficial owner difficult to establish): decision of the Chief Executive Officer after the written opinion of the RCCI (N2);
- residence in a country subject to a FATF call for countermeasures: reasoned decision of the Chief Executive Officer after the RCCI opinion, and information of the Chairman of the Board within 5 business days.

This policy adds one rule: where the client is linked to the Chief Executive Officer, a director or a shareholder, the decision falls to level N3, the person concerned abstaining.

### Article 16 — Issuers and professional counterparties

Before any commitment with an issuer, a co-arranger, a syndicate member, a settlement bank or another intermediary, the desk verifies its identity, its licence where applicable, its beneficial owners and the absence of sanctions. The RCCI gives an opinion where the counterparty is established outside CEMAC or presents a high risk. The decision to enter into the relationship follows the level of the intended transaction.

### Article 17 — New products, new activities, new markets

Any product, service, distribution channel, client category, market or country in which the Company does not yet operate is treated as new, as is any significant change to an existing product.

No launch takes place without:

1. the risk analysis using the form in the Risk Management Policy (PC-POL-RIS-001, Appendix 5), prepared by the project sponsor;
2. the written opinion of the RCCI, stating in particular whether the licensed business plan is modified;
3. verification of the prerequisites in the checklist (Appendix 4): procedures, contracts, systems, training, client information;
4. the decision of the Chief Executive Officer (N2) or, where the licensed business plan is modified or the activity commits own funds beyond the limits, of the Board of Directors (N4), which then authorises the application for prior approval to COSUMAF.

The Board is informed of any launch decided at level N2 at its next meeting. Six months after the launch, the RCCI makes a written review (incidents, complaints, compliance with the conditions set), presented to the risk committee.

### Article 18 — Significant outsourcing

All outsourcing follows the Outsourcing Policy (PC-POL-EXT-001, Articles 8 to 10): qualification by the RCCI, risk analysis, due diligence on the service provider, written opinion of the RCCI, then decision of the Chief Executive Officer (N2) or, for a critical or important function, of the Board of Directors (N4). This policy adds nothing to it.

## Title V — Client orders and own account

### Article 19 — Client orders: what falls under the procedure

The checks prior to accepting an order (up-to-date file, no alert, consistency, suitability to the profile, cover) are set by the Order Reception, Transmission and Execution Procedure (PC-PRO-ORD-001, Articles 7 and 8). An order that passes these checks requires no further approval. This title deals only with exceptional orders.

### Article 20 — Exceptional orders

| Situation | Rule | Who approves | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Order of FCFA 50,000,000 or more, or exceeding the usual liquidity of the security | Second review before transmission: consistency with the profile and the source of funds, proposal to split the order | Head of Capital Markets, in addition to the desk (N1) | Sign-off on the order form or in the Guichet |
| Order of FCFA 200,000,000 or more, or liable to cause the client to cross a holding disclosure threshold | In addition, RCCI opinion before transmission | RCCI, then Head of Capital Markets | Written opinion in the order file |
| Order without full cover | **Prohibited.** No approval level can authorise it (PC-PRO-ORD-001, Article 8) | — | — |
| Order on an account under review: overdue file update, screening alert, enhanced examination in progress | Order suspended. Only a sell order or a withdrawal to the declared bank account may be authorised, if it protects the client from a loss | Chief Executive Officer after the written opinion of the RCCI (N2) | Commitment request (Appendix 2) |
| Order on an account subject to a suspicious transaction report under analysis | The RCCI decides alone, respecting confidentiality (PC-POL-LBC-001, Article 25) | RCCI | AML/CFT file |
| Order on a frozen account | **Prohibited** (PC-POL-LBC-001, Article 22) | — | — |
| Order on a security on the restricted list or the watch list | Prior check | RCCI | Order register |

### Article 21 — Own account and cash investment

The Company carries out **no own-account transactions** other than the investment of its cash, in accordance with its risk appetite statement (PC-POL-RIS-001, Appendix 1).

Cash investments are limited to deposits with banks licensed in CEMAC and CEMAC government securities with a residual maturity of two years or less (PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 14). Each investment or withdrawal is decided by the Chief Executive Officer and executed under dual signature in accordance with PC-COD-004. Any other investment falls to the Board of Directors (N4).

Own-account orders are time-stamped, transmitted after pending client orders on the same security, and checked each month by the RCCI.

## Title VI — Payments and financial commitments

### Article 22 — Principles

The Company's payments and financial commitments follow the delegation and signature matrix of the Organisation and Delegation of Powers Charter (PC-COD-004) and the Accounting Procedures Manual (PC-PRO-CPT-001). They comply with the following principles:

1. **Segregation**: the person who commits the expenditure, the person who checks and records it, and the person who signs the payment are not the same person; with limited staff, at least two people are involved in each payment.
2. **Dual signature** above the threshold set by PC-COD-004; no payment is signed by its beneficiary.
3. **Supporting document**: no payment without an approved and recorded invoice or document.
4. **Bank details**: any new beneficiary, or any change of a beneficiary's bank account, is confirmed by a telephone call to a number already known before the first payment.
5. **Client funds**: they are never used to pay an expense of the Company; their movements follow the Segregation and Reconciliation of Client Assets Procedure (PC-PRO-SEG-001).

### Article 23 — Commitments reserved to the Board

The following always require a prior resolution of the Board of Directors (N4):

- any unbudgeted expenditure or commitment exceeding 5% of reference own funds (PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 13);
- any surety, endorsement or guarantee given by the Company;
- any borrowing and any security interest over the Company's assets;
- any related-party agreement within the meaning of the OHADA Uniform Act on Commercial Companies and Economic Interest Groups;
- any transaction exceeding the powers delegated to the Chief Executive Officer (PC-COD-002, Article 4).

## Title VII — Traceability, control and reporting

### Article 24 — Approval file

Any approval at levels N2 to N4 is based on a file comprising at least:

- the commitment request (Appendix 2), dated and signed by the sponsor;
- the analysis documents: presentation of the transaction, know-your-customer checks on the client or counterparty, non-placement scenario for a guarantee, new product form where applicable;
- the written opinion of the RCCI;
- the decision at the required level: sign-off of the Chief Executive Officer, minutes of the commitment committee (Appendix 3), written consent of the Chairman of the Board, extract from the Board minutes;
- the signed contract or deed, dated after the decision.

The file is compiled by the sponsor of the transaction and handed to the RCCI within 5 business days of signature.

### Article 25 — Register and retention

The RCCI keeps the register of commitments and approvals (Appendix 5). Each entry states the transaction, the amount, the required level, the RCCI opinion, the decision and its author, and the outcome.

The files and the register are retained for at least **ten years** from the settlement of the transaction, in accordance with the Archiving and Retention Procedure (PC-PRO-ARC-001). They are provided to COSUMAF, the statutory auditors and the independent review provider on request.

### Article 26 — Ex-post control by the RCCI

The RCCI includes the following controls in the annual control plan (PC-COD-003, Article 15):

- **each month**: reconciliation of signed contracts, significant payments and exceptional orders with the register, to ensure that no commitment was made without approval or at the wrong level;
- **each quarter**: review of a sample of approval files; follow-up of the conditions set in the RCCI's opinions;
- **whenever a guarantee is called**: review of the outcome and of the disposal plan.

Any commitment made without approval, or at the wrong level, is reported as an incident under the Risk Management Policy (PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 28) and brought to the attention of the Chairman of the Board within 5 business days.

### Article 27 — Reporting to the Board

At each quarterly meeting, together with the risk dashboard, the Chief Executive Officer presents to the Board of Directors:

- the list of commitments approved at levels N2 and N3 since the previous meeting;
- the outstanding firm underwriting and guarantee commitments, in amount and as a percentage of own funds;
- the new products and activities launched;
- decisions taken despite an unfavourable RCCI opinion.

In the annual report to the Board, the RCCI presents the results of the controls, approval incidents and recommendations, and proposes, where appropriate, adjustments to the thresholds in Appendix 1.

## Title VIII — Final provisions

### Article 28 — Breaches

Making a commitment without the required approval, splitting a transaction to stay below a threshold, or backdating an approval constitutes misconduct exposing its author to the sanctions provided for in Article 49 of the Code of Conduct, without prejudice to COSUMAF sanctions.

The limits set by this policy are binding on Staff Members and the Chief Executive Officer in their relations with the Company, even though they are not enforceable against third parties.

### Article 29 — Review and entry into force

The policy is reviewed at least once a year by the RCCI, with the Chief Executive Officer, and whenever there is a significant change in activities, own funds or regulation. The thresholds in Appendix 1 are reviewed at the same time as the table of limits of the Risk Management Policy. Any amendment is approved by the Board of Directors.

This policy enters into force on the date of its adoption by the Board of Directors, as shown in the document control sheet.
