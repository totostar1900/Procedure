## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This Code sets out the ethical and conduct rules applicable to Purpose Capital S.A. Its aim is to safeguard market integrity, investor protection and the reputation of the Company.

### Article 2 — Legal basis

The Code falls within the framework of the following texts, which it supplements without replacing them:

- Regulation No. 01/22/CEMAC/UMAC/CM/COSUMAF of 21 July 2022 on the organisation and functioning of the Central African financial market;
- COSUMAF General Regulation of 23 May 2023 and its implementing instructions;
- Regulation No. 02/24/CEMAC/UMAC/CM of 20 December 2024 on the prevention and suppression of money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing in Central Africa, and Regulation No. 04/24/CEMAC/UMAC/CM of the same date on targeted financial sanctions;
- BVMAC market rules and the general regulation of the central securities depository;
- OHADA Uniform Act on the law of commercial companies and economic interest groups;
- Cameroonian Labour Code and the Company's internal rules.

In the event of conflict, the more stringent rule applies.

### Article 3 — Persons subject to the Code

The Code applies to any person acting on behalf of the Company (the "Staff Members"):

- directors, the Chief Executive Officer and Deputy Chief Executive Officers;
- permanent employees, fixed-term staff, interns and temporary staff;
- traders, account managers, analysts and client relationship officers;
- service providers and agents with access to the Company's systems, premises or information.

### Article 4 — Definitions

The terms *inside information*, *personal transaction*, *beneficial owner*, *politically exposed person* and *RCCI* have the meaning given in the glossary of the Company's document repository, which forms an integral part of this Code.

## Title II — Fundamental principles

### Article 5 — Primacy of the client's interest

Staff Members place the client's interest before that of the Company and before their own personal interest. No commercial objective justifies a breach of this principle.

### Article 6 — Integrity and fairness

Staff Members act honestly, fairly and transparently towards clients, issuers, counterparties and the authorities. They refrain from any misleading practice, any promise of guaranteed returns and any distorted presentation of risks.

### Article 7 — Due care and competence

Each Staff Member performs their duties with care and maintains the competence required by their position. Persons performing regulated functions hold the professional cards or authorisations required by COSUMAF and BVMAC.

### Article 8 — Neutrality and impartiality

Clients are treated fairly, without favouritism. No client receives information or order handling to the detriment of another.

### Article 9 — Market integrity

Staff Members contribute to the regular, transparent and orderly functioning of the Regional Financial Market and refrain from any conduct likely to undermine confidence in it.

### Article 10 — Leadership by example

Directors and senior managers set an example, allocate sufficient resources to compliance and never put pressure on a Staff Member to depart from this Code.

## Title III — Client relations

### Article 11 — Client onboarding

No account is opened without a signed account agreement, full identification of the client and of the beneficial owner, and a documented investor profile (financial situation, objectives, investment horizon, risk tolerance, knowledge).

### Article 12 — Suitability of advice

All advice or recommendations are suited to the client's profile. Where a transaction requested does not match the profile, the Staff Member warns the client in writing before execution.

### Article 13 — Client information

Information provided is clear, accurate and not misleading. It presents risks as prominently as benefits, together with all applicable fees, commissions and taxes, in accordance with the published schedule of charges.

### Article 14 — Reception and execution of orders

Orders are:

1. time-stamped and recorded upon receipt, whatever the channel (counter, recorded telephone line, electronic platform);
2. transmitted to BVMAC in the chronological order of receipt;
3. executed on the best possible terms, taking into account price, cost, speed and likelihood of execution;
4. confirmed to the client by a contract note within the regulatory time limits.

Aggregated orders are allocated according to a fair allocation rule set in advance. Detailed arrangements are set out in the Best Execution Policy (PC-POL-EXE-001).

### Article 15 — Prohibitions

It is prohibited to:

- place an order without a mandate or beyond the mandate received;
- carry out excessive transactions solely to generate commissions (churning);
- execute an order for the Company's own account or a personal account ahead of a client order in the same security (front running);
- guarantee a client against losses or share in their gains.

### Article 16 — Segregation of client assets

Clients' securities and cash are kept strictly separate from those of the Company, identified client by client and reconciled at least monthly with the statements of the central securities depository and of the settlement bank. Their use for the Company's own account is prohibited.

### Article 17 — Complaints

Every complaint is recorded, acknowledged within 2 business days and dealt with within 30 days at most. The complaints register is kept by the RCCI and provided to COSUMAF on request. The client is informed of their right to refer the matter to COSUMAF, which has mediation powers.

## Title IV — Conflicts of interest

### Article 18 — Principle

The Company identifies, prevents and manages situations in which its interest, that of a Staff Member or that of a client may conflict with the interest of another client. Where a conflict cannot be managed, it is disclosed to the client before any transaction, or the transaction is declined. Detailed arrangements for identification, prevention and management are set out in the Conflicts of Interest Policy (PC-POL-CI-001).

### Article 19 — Conflicts map and register

The RCCI keeps a map of potential conflicts (placement of an issue for an issuer advised by the Company, dealing on own account, shareholding links, directorships) and a register of actual conflicts and of the measures taken.

### Article 20 — Segregation of functions (Chinese walls)

Issuer advisory and placement activities are separated from dealing, research and management activities on behalf of clients: segregated IT access, separate files, prohibition on passing on sensitive information other than on a "need-to-know" basis. Any wall crossing is authorised in advance and recorded by the RCCI.

### Article 21 — Declaration of interests

Each Staff Member declares, on recruitment and annually thereafter, their corporate offices, significant shareholdings and outside activities (Appendix 3). Any paid outside activity requires the prior authorisation of Executive Management.

### Article 22 — Gifts and benefits

A Staff Member may not solicit any gift or benefit. They may accept a customary gift worth less than FCFA 50,000; above that amount, they declare it to the RCCI, who decides how it is to be dealt with. Cash, loans and benefits linked to a pending decision are prohibited whatever the amount.

### Article 23 — Remuneration

The remuneration policy does not create incentives contrary to clients' interests; the variable portion is not based solely on the volume of commissions.

## Title V — Inside information and market abuse

### Article 24 — Insider dealing prohibition

Any Staff Member in possession of inside information refrains from: carrying out a transaction in the security concerned, for themselves or for others; recommending or inducing a third party to do so; disclosing the information outside the normal course of their duties.

### Article 25 — Insider lists and watch list

For each sensitive transaction (stock market listing, bond issue, corporate finance transaction of an issuer), the RCCI keeps a list naming the persons with access to the information, with the date and time of their inclusion. The securities concerned are placed on a restricted list for personal transactions for the entire duration of the transaction.

### Article 26 — Market manipulation

The following are prohibited: fictitious or matched orders intended to give a misleading impression of supply, demand or price; the dissemination of rumours or false information; any arrangement aimed at artificially fixing a price.

### Article 27 — Personal transactions

Staff Members' personal transactions:

1. are carried out exclusively through Purpose Capital S.A., unless the RCCI grants a written exemption;
2. are subject to a prior authorisation request to the RCCI, valid for 48 hours;
3. are held for at least 30 days before resale, save in exceptional circumstances that have been authorised;
4. are never executed ahead of a pending client order in the same security;
5. are declared in a signed quarterly statement (Appendix 2).

### Article 28 — Blackout periods

No Staff Member deals in a security for which the Company holds an advisory or placement mandate, nor during the 15 days preceding the publication of the results of an issuer covered by the Company.

### Article 29 — Reporting suspicious transactions

Any Staff Member who suspects market abuse, whether by a client or by a colleague, reports it without delay to the RCCI, who assesses whether COSUMAF should be informed.

## Title VI — Confidentiality and data protection

### Article 30 — Professional secrecy

Staff Members are bound by secrecy regarding the identity, assets, orders and transactions of clients, as well as information relating to the Company and to issuers. This secrecy may only be lifted with respect to COSUMAF, ANIF, the judicial authorities and other authorities empowered by law. It continues to apply after the end of the contract.

### Article 31 — Personal data

Client data are collected for specified purposes, limited to what is necessary, stored securely and accessible only to authorised persons, in accordance with Cameroonian legislation in force on the protection of personal data.

### Article 32 — Systems security

Each Staff Member protects their login credentials, locks their workstation, uses only tools approved by the Company and does not extract any client data to a personal email account or storage device. Any incident (loss of equipment, suspicious access, data leak) is reported immediately to Executive Management and to the RCCI.

### Article 33 — Recorded communications

Communications relating to orders (telephone, business email) go exclusively through the Company's recorded channels. The use of personal messaging services to receive or transmit orders is prohibited, unless the tool has been approved and is archived by the Company.

## Title VII — Anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism

### Article 34 — Risk-based approach

The Company classifies each client by risk level (low, standard, high) according to their profile, country, source of funds and the products used, in accordance with CEMAC Regulation No. 02/24 of 20 December 2024 and COSUMAF instructions on AML/CFT. Detailed arrangements are set out in the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Policy (PC-POL-LBC-001).

### Article 35 — Due diligence

Staff Members:

- verify the identity of the client and of the beneficial owner against official documents before any transaction;
- apply enhanced due diligence to politically exposed persons, non-resident clients, complex or unusual transactions and large cash deposits;
- screen clients and counterparties against the applicable sanctions lists;
- update the client file at least every 2 years, and annually for high-risk clients.

### Article 36 — Suspicious transaction report

Any suspicion is escalated without delay to the designated AML/CFT officer, who decides whether to file a report with ANIF, the sole recipient of suspicious transaction reports. The report is made in writing. Any new information likely to confirm or dispel the suspicion is sent to ANIF without delay.

### Article 37 — Prohibition of tipping off

It is strictly prohibited to inform the client or any third party of the existence of a suspicious transaction report or of an ongoing investigation.

### Article 38 — Record retention

Identification documents and records relating to transactions are retained for at least 10 years after the end of the relationship or the execution of the transaction.

## Title VIII — Relations with the authorities, the market and the public

### Article 39 — Cooperation with the authorities

The Company and its Staff Members respond accurately, completely and promptly to requests from COSUMAF, BVMAC, the central securities depository and ANIF, and facilitate on-site and desk-based inspections. Any concealment or obstruction constitutes gross misconduct.

### Article 40 — Regulatory reporting

Periodic returns (financial statements, prudential ratios, activity statistics, RCCI annual report) are submitted on time and under the responsibility of Executive Management.

### Article 41 — Relations with peers

The Company maintains fair relations with other brokerage firms (sociétés de bourse) and intermediaries. It refrains from disparagement, unfair poaching of clients and any arrangement contrary to free competition.

### Article 42 — Communication and advertising

Any marketing communication (website, social media, brochures, media appearances):

- is approved in advance by the RCCI;
- identifies Purpose Capital S.A. as a brokerage firm licensed by COSUMAF and states its licence number;
- uses no name or description suggesting the status of a bank or investment bank;
- does not display past performance without stating that it is not a guide to future performance.

### Article 43 — Public statements

Only the Chairman of the Board of Directors, the Chief Executive Officer or the persons they designate speak on behalf of the Company. Staff Members refrain from publishing on social media any recommendation on a security or any information about a client or an issuer.

## Title IX — Compliance framework

### Article 44 — The RCCI

The Board of Directors appoints a Head of Compliance and Internal Control, whose appointment is notified to COSUMAF. The RCCI is independent of operational and commercial functions, has unrestricted access to information and reports directly to the Chief Executive Officer and to the Board of Directors (or to its audit and risk committee).

### Article 45 — Duties of the RCCI

The RCCI:

- ensures that this Code is applied and proposes updates to it;
- keeps the registers (conflicts of interest, insider lists, personal transactions, gifts, complaints);
- authorises personal transactions and approves marketing communications;
- unless a separate appointment is made, acts as AML/CFT officer in dealings with ANIF;
- carries out an annual control plan and presents an annual compliance report to the Board.

### Article 46 — Training

Every new Staff Member receives training on this Code within their first month. Refresher training, including AML/CFT and market abuse, is provided at least once a year; attendance is mandatory and recorded.

### Article 47 — Whistleblowing

Any Staff Member may report, in good faith, a breach of this Code or of the regulations to the RCCI or, where the RCCI is involved, to the Chairman of the Board of Directors. The whistleblower's identity is protected; no sanction or retaliatory measure may be taken against them on account of their report.

### Article 48 — Questions

In case of doubt about the conduct to adopt, the Staff Member consults the RCCI before acting. The absence of an explicit rule does not constitute authorisation.

## Title X — Sanctions and final provisions

### Article 49 — Internal sanctions

Any breach of this Code constitutes misconduct which may lead, depending on its seriousness and in compliance with the Labour Code and the internal rules, to: a warning, a reprimand, suspension, demotion or dismissal for gross misconduct. These measures are without prejudice to disciplinary sanctions imposed by COSUMAF or to civil or criminal proceedings.

### Article 50 — Adherence

Each Staff Member receives the Code against a signed receipt, signs the declaration of adherence (Appendix 1) and renews it at each update. The Code is annexed to the internal rules and to employment and service contracts.

### Article 51 — Review

The Code is reviewed at least once a year by the RCCI and upon each significant regulatory change. Any amendment is approved by the Board of Directors.

### Article 52 — Entry into force

This Code enters into force on the date of its adoption by the Board of Directors, as shown in the document control sheet. It is communicated to COSUMAF.
