## Title I — General provisions

### Article 1 — Purpose

This manual sets out how Purpose Capital S.A. (the "Company") keeps its accounts and manages its money: who records, who authorises, who pays, who checks, by when and with what evidence.

It has four aims: faithful accounts; money protected against error and fraud; tax, social security and regulatory obligations met on time; own funds known every month and kept above the minimum on which the licence depends.

### Article 2 — Scope

The manual applies to all financial transactions of the Company on its own account: revenue, expenditure, payroll, cash and treasury, fixed assets, taxes, closings and financial statements.

It applies to anyone involved in them, whether employee or service provider, including the chartered accountant.

Client securities and cash are not assets of the Company. Their segregation and reconciliation are governed by the Procedure for the Segregation and Reconciliation of Client Assets (PC-PRO-SEG-001); this manual only states how they appear in the accounts (Article 7).

### Article 3 — Definitions

- **Accounting**: the person who keeps the Company's accounts, either an accountant employed by the Company or a chartered accountant registered with the National Order of Chartered Accountants of Cameroon (ONECCA) acting under an engagement letter.
- **Authorising officer**: the person who authorises an expense or a payment within the limits of their authority.
- **Signatory**: the person authorised by the bank to validate a transfer or sign a cheque.
- **Supporting document**: the document that evidences a transaction: invoice, contract, contract note, bank statement, payslip, return.
- **Own funds**: the Company's equity calculated in accordance with Article 31.

### Article 4 — Relationship with other documents

| Subject | Reference document |
| --- | --- |
| Authority, spending limits and bank signatures | Organisation and Delegation of Authority Charter (PC-COD-004) |
| Client assets, segregated settlement account | PC-PRO-SEG-001 |
| Own funds, liquidity and investment limits | Risk Management Policy (PC-POL-RIS-001) |
| Returns sent to COSUMAF and other authorities | Regulatory Reporting Procedure (PC-PRO-REP-001) |
| Use of a chartered accountant or payroll provider | Outsourcing Policy (PC-POL-EXT-001) |
| Commissions invoiced to issuers and other firms | Contract templates PC-CTR-001, PC-CTR-002 and PC-CTR-003 |
| Charges invoiced to clients | Securities Account Opening Agreement (PC-CTR-004, Article 6 and Appendix 1) |
| Remuneration of Staff Members and senior managers | Remuneration Policy (PC-POL-REM-001) |
| Retention of books and supporting documents | Archiving and Retention Procedure (PC-PRO-ARC-001) |

In the event of any difference, the reference document prevails on its subject.

## Title II — Accounting framework

### Article 5 — Framework

The Company keeps its accounts in accordance with the OHADA Uniform Act on Accounting Law and Financial Reporting and the revised OHADA Accounting System (SYSCOHADA), **full system**. In practice:

- **accrual accounting**: revenue is recorded when earned and an expense when incurred, whatever the payment date;
- **financial year** from 1 January to 31 December, accounts kept in CFA francs;
- **mandatory books**: journal, general ledger, trial balance and inventory book, kept in software compliant with SYSCOHADA;
- **annual financial statements**: balance sheet, income statement, cash flow statement and notes.

If COSUMAF requires brokerage firms to use a particular presentation or particular statements, these are prepared in addition, from the same accounts.

### Article 6 — The Company's chart of accounts

Accounting maintains the Company's chart of accounts, derived from the SYSCOHADA chart. It includes at least separate sub-accounts for:

- each type of revenue: brokerage commissions, arrangement, placement, success, underwriting and listing commissions, custody fees, other;
- retrocessions paid to members of a placement syndicate;
- fees and contributions due to COSUMAF, BVMAC and the Central Securities Depository;
- each bank account, each cash investment and petty cash;
- client cash and the corresponding liability to clients (Article 7).

Any new account is signed off by the Chief Executive Officer.

### Article 7 — Client cash and securities in the accounts

1. **Client securities** do not belong to the Company and do not appear on its balance sheet. They are tracked in the Guichet registers and with the account keepers (PC-PRO-SEG-001).
2. **Client cash** held in the segregated settlement account is recorded in a cash account specific to that bank account and, as the counterpart, in a liability account "clients — cash holdings". These two accounts are never offset against the Company's accounts.
3. This cash is **excluded** from the calculation of the Company's own funds and own cash.
4. A commission owed by a client is recorded as revenue only once due, and is transferred to a Company account only in accordance with Article 7 of PC-PRO-SEG-001.
5. Each month, the book balance of the liability to clients is reconciled with the total of the balances in the client cash ledger (Article 27).

### Article 8 — Supporting documents and audit trail

- **No entry without a supporting document.** Each entry refers to a supporting document that is scanned and filed in the Company's document storage.
- Invoices issued are numbered in a continuous chronological sequence, with no gaps or duplicates.
- An error is corrected by a reversing entry, never by deletion. The software prevents any change to a closed period.
- Any manual entry on a cash, revenue or client liability account bears the name of its author and its justification.

## Title III — Organisation of the accounting function

### Article 9 — Who does what

| Function | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Board of Directors | Approves the budget; approves the annual accounts for submission; receives the quarterly financial report; authorises related-party agreements |
| Chairman of the Board of Directors | Approves the expense claims and personal expenses of the Chief Executive Officer; second signatory above the thresholds set by PC-COD-004 |
| Chief Executive Officer | Principal authorising officer; bank signatory; oversees Accounting; signs off closings; signs tax and social security returns |
| Head of Capital Markets, Head of Sales | Commit the expenses of their area within the limits of PC-COD-004; certify that services have been received; provide the invoicing details for their transactions |
| Desk (operations) | Produces contract notes and the calculation of brokerage commissions; provides the monthly client asset reconciliation |
| Accounting | Records, invoices, prepares payments, payroll, returns, reconciliations, closings, financial statements and the own funds calculation; **has no bank signing authority** |
| RCCI | Checks compliance with the manual; checks the own funds calculation and prudential returns; takes part neither in the authorisation nor in the execution of payments |
| Statutory auditors | Certify the annual accounts; take no part in bookkeeping |

### Article 10 — Segregation of duties with a small team

No one carries out alone all four stages of a transaction: **record, authorise, pay, reconcile**. With few people:

1. Accounting records and prepares, but signs no payment.
2. The authorising officer authorises, but does not record.
3. Every payment goes through two people: an initiator and a validator, in accordance with Article 21.
4. The bank reconciliation is prepared by Accounting and reviewed by a person who did not post the entries.
5. For transactions concerning the Chief Executive Officer personally (remuneration, expense claims, agreements with a related company), the second level is the Chairman of the Board of Directors.
6. Where the absence of a person prevents segregation, the transaction waits for their return or goes through the deputy designated in PC-COD-004; it is reported to the RCCI.

### Article 11 — External chartered accountant

Where bookkeeping is entrusted to a chartered accountant:

- a written **engagement letter** sets out the work (bookkeeping, reconciliations, payroll, returns, closings, financial statements, DSF), deadlines, confidentiality, access to data and its return at the end of the engagement; it complies with the clauses of PC-POL-EXT-001 and the contract is entered in the outsourcing register;
- the books and supporting documents remain the property of the Company, in software or storage to which it always has access;
- the chartered accountant has **no access** with write rights to the bank accounts or online payment platforms;
- the Chief Executive Officer **oversees** the work: they receive the monthly closing file, raise their questions in writing and sign off the closing (Appendix 1);
- each year, the Chief Executive Officer assesses the service in accordance with PC-POL-EXT-001; the assessment is given to the RCCI.

Responsibility for the accounts remains with the Company and its senior managers, even where bookkeeping is entrusted to a third party.

## Title IV — Revenue cycle

### Article 12 — Sources of revenue and recognition date

| Revenue | Basis | Recognised as revenue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brokerage commission and transaction fees | Charges given to the client (PC-CTR-004, Appendix 1); automatic calculation on the contract note | On the order execution date |
| Custody and account-keeping fees, if any | Charges given to the client | Pro rata over the period covered |
| Arrangement commission | Mandate agreement (PC-CTR-001, Article 12) | As the agreed milestones are reached (signature, COSUMAF approval (visa)) |
| Placement commission | PC-CTR-001, Article 12; PC-CTR-003, Article 15 | On the settlement date, on the amounts subscribed and paid up |
| Success commission | PC-CTR-001, Article 12 | When the condition is met, on the settlement date |
| Underwriting commission | Firm underwriting agreement (PC-CTR-002, Article 14) | According to the due dates set by the agreement |
| Listing commission | PC-CTR-001, Article 12 | On the admission date |

An amount invoiced before the service is rendered is recorded as deferred income. Retrocessions due to members of a placement syndicate are recorded as an expense on the same date as the corresponding commission, without offsetting.

### Article 13 — Invoicing

- **Brokerage clients**: commissions appear on each contract note. Each month, Accounting records the total produced by the Guichet and reconciles it with the commissions actually transferred from the segregated account to the Company's account.
- **Issuers and other firms**: the director in charge of the transaction gives Accounting, within **five business days** of the triggering event, the signed agreement and the calculation; Accounting issues the invoice, signed by the Chief Executive Officer.
- Each invoice bears the legal and tax particulars, including the unique identification number (NIU) of both parties, taxes and the due date.
- An invoice is never cancelled: it is corrected by a numbered credit note, signed off by the Chief Executive Officer.

### Article 14 — Collection and follow-up of receivables

Accounting keeps the aged receivables balance and gives it to the Chief Executive Officer each month.

| Days overdue | Action | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 days | Written reminder | Accounting |
| 30 days | Reminder by the director in charge of the client | Director concerned |
| 60 days | Formal notice; consideration of a payment arrangement | Chief Executive Officer |
| 90 days | Provision for doubtful debt; decision on legal recovery | Accounting, on the decision of the Chief Executive Officer |

A receivable is written off only on the written decision of the Chief Executive Officer, once reasonable remedies have been exhausted; above FCFA 1,000,000, the Board of Directors is informed in the following quarter.

## Title V — Purchases and expenses cycle

### Article 15 — Committing expenditure

1. Every expense is **provided for in the budget**. An unbudgeted expense is authorised by the Chief Executive Officer; above 5% of own funds, it is submitted to the Board of Directors (PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 13).
2. The requester obtains the agreement of the competent authorising officer under PC-COD-004 **before** placing the order.
3. From **FCFA 2,000,000 excluding tax**, at least two quotes are requested, unless a sole supplier is justified in writing.
4. A contract with a director, a senior manager, a shareholder or a company related to them follows the related-party agreement procedure of the Uniform Act on Commercial Companies and the Conflicts of Interest Management Policy (PC-POL-CI-001).
5. Using a service provider for a function of the Company also follows PC-POL-EXT-001.

### Article 16 — Checking the supplier invoice

On receipt, Accounting checks that the invoice matches an authorised order or contract, that **receipt of the service** has been certified by the requester, and that the amounts, taxes and legal particulars are correct. It records the invoice on receipt, even if payment is later. A non-compliant invoice is returned to the supplier, never corrected by hand.

### Article 17 — Expense claims

- Expenses incurred for the Company are reimbursed on an expense claim with the original receipts, submitted within **thirty days**.
- It is approved by the line manager; that of the Chief Executive Officer by the Chairman of the Board of Directors.
- Reimbursement is made by transfer, with the payroll or separately; never in cash above the limit in Article 18.
- Gifts and hospitality also follow the Code of Conduct (PC-COD-001, Article 22).

### Article 18 — Petty cash

The Company keeps cash to a strict minimum:

- **petty cash limit: FCFA 200,000**; **cash expense: FCFA 50,000 at most** per transaction;
- a single cash custodian, designated in writing by the Chief Executive Officer, who is not the person keeping the cash accounts where Accounting is in-house;
- each withdrawal has its signed voucher and supporting document; no loan or personal advance is made from petty cash;
- petty cash is replenished by transfer or cheque, on presentation of the supporting documents for the period;
- it is counted at each month end by the custodian and a second Staff Member, who sign the count report; the RCCI carries out a surprise count at least once every six months;
- client cash never passes through petty cash.

### Article 19 — Supplier master file

Creating a supplier and any change to its bank details are carried out by Accounting at the written request of the supplier. A change of bank details is **confirmed by telephone** on the number already known, by a person other than the one who received the request, before any payment; the confirmation is noted on file. A pressing request is treated as an attempted fraud until proven otherwise.

## Title VI — Treasury and payments

### Article 20 — Bank accounts

Accounting keeps the list of accounts opened in the Company's name, with their purpose, the bank and the signatories. It distinguishes at least:

| Account | Purpose | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company current account | Day-to-day receipts and payments | Payments in accordance with Article 21 |
| Investment account or deposit | Surplus cash | Movements only to the Company's current account; dual validation always |
| Segregated settlement account | Client cash | Governed by PC-PRO-SEG-001; dual validation always |
| Own securities accounts, if any | Investments in government securities | PC-PRO-SEG-001, Article 4; PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 14 |

Opening or closing an account and any change of signatory are decided in accordance with PC-COD-004; the RCCI and the statutory auditors are informed. Online banking credentials are personal and never shared.

### Article 21 — Authorisation and execution of payments

1. Each payment is prepared by Accounting using the payment request in Appendix 2, together with the checked invoice or the document that justifies it.
2. It is **authorised** by the competent authorising officer, then **executed** by two separate signatories: an initiator and a validator in online banking, or two signatures on a cheque.
3. The **thresholds** above which a specific second signatory (in particular the Chairman of the Board of Directors) is required are those of the delegation matrix of PC-COD-004.
4. Transfer is the normal means of payment; cheques are the exception; cash payment is limited by Article 18.
5. No payment is made on a mere copy, reminder or email; an invoice already paid is marked "paid" to avoid double payment.
6. Taxes, social security contributions and regulatory fees are paid when due, without waiting for a reminder.

### Article 22 — Bank reconciliations

- **Each month**, for each Company account, Accounting reconciles the bank statement balance with the book balance, using Appendix 3, no later than the **tenth business day** of the following month.
- Every difference is explained; an unexplained difference or an item outstanding for more than one month is reported to the Chief Executive Officer.
- The reconciliation is **reviewed and signed** by the Chief Executive Officer, who checks by sampling that the month's payments correspond to authorised requests.
- The segregated settlement account is reconciled in accordance with PC-PRO-SEG-001; Accounting checks that this reconciliation agrees with its accounts.

### Article 23 — Cash forecasting and investment of own funds

- Accounting keeps a **twelve-month cash plan**, updated each month with expected receipts and payments, and presents it to the risk and compliance committee.
- Own cash is invested **prudently**, in accordance with PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 14: deposits with banks licensed in CEMAC and CEMAC government securities with a residual maturity of two years or less, without exceeding the bank concentration limit of that policy.
- Every investment is decided by the Chief Executive Officer and executed in accordance with Article 21; the Company never speculates on its own account.
- Client cash is never used for the Company's needs, not even for one day.

## Title VII — Fixed assets, payroll and taxation

### Article 24 — Fixed assets

- A durable asset worth at least **FCFA 250,000 excluding tax** is capitalised; below that, it is expensed.
- Accounting keeps the **fixed asset register**: description, date, cost, depreciation period, location, user. Each asset bears a label.
- Depreciation is straight-line, over useful lives set by Accounting and signed off by the Chief Executive Officer, for example: IT equipment 3 years, furniture 10 years, fit-out 10 years or the lease term, software 3 years.
- A physical inventory is carried out each year at 31 December; a missing or unusable asset is removed from the register on the decision of the Chief Executive Officer.

### Article 25 — Payroll

1. The Chief Executive Officer sends Accounting, no later than the **20th of the month**, the variable items: joiners, leavers, absences, hours, bonuses decided under PC-POL-REM-001.
2. Accounting prepares the payslips and a control report explaining any change of more than 5% compared with the previous month.
3. The Chief Executive Officer checks and approves the payroll; their own remuneration is that set by the Board of Directors, and any change is signed off by the Chairman of the Board.
4. Salaries are paid by transfer before the end of the month, in accordance with Article 21. Each Staff Member receives their payslip.

### Article 26 — Tax and social security obligations

Accounting prepares, and the Chief Executive Officer signs and has paid, the Company's returns:

| Deadline | Return |
| --- | --- |
| Each month, no later than the 15th | Output and input VAT; corporate income tax instalment; taxes and deductions on salaries; CNPS contributions; withholding taxes due by the Company (rent, services from non-residents, other cases provided for by the General Tax Code) |
| Each year | Business licence tax (patente); Statistical and Tax Return (DSF) with the financial statements and the balance of corporate income tax; annual salary return |
| As provided by the texts | Fees and contributions due to COSUMAF, BVMAC and the Central Securities Depository |

Details, dates and responsibilities are set out in the calendar in Appendix 4, updated by Accounting each year after publication of the finance law and signed off by the Chief Executive Officer. Tax assessments, tax or social security audits and any reassessment are communicated without delay to the Chief Executive Officer and the RCCI.

## Title VIII — Closings

### Article 27 — Monthly closing

Each month, Accounting closes the accounts for the past month using the checklist in Appendix 1, no later than the **tenth business day** of the following month. It includes at least:

- recording of all invoices issued and received, payroll and returns;
- bank reconciliations (Article 22) and the petty cash count (Article 18);
- reconciliation of the liability to clients with the cash ledger, and a check that the full monthly reconciliation of client assets (PC-PRO-SEG-001, Article 11) has been carried out with no unexplained difference;
- accrued expenses, accrued income and deferred income;
- depreciation for the month;
- clearing of suspense accounts;
- the own funds calculation and the dashboard in Appendix 5 (Article 31).

The Chief Executive Officer signs off the checklist. The closing file is kept with the supporting documents.

### Article 28 — Annual closing and financial statements

At 31 December, in addition to the monthly closing, Accounting:

- carries out the inventory of petty cash, fixed assets and investments;
- obtains confirmation of balances from banks and account keepers, at the request of the statutory auditors;
- assesses **provisions**: doubtful debts, litigation, paid leave, end-of-service benefits and retirement obligations required by the revised SYSCOHADA;
- calculates corporate income tax;
- prepares the financial statements and the DSF.

| Step | Latest date | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Provisional accounts given to the Chief Executive Officer | 28 February | Accounting |
| Filing of the DSF | 15 March, or date set by the finance law | Accounting; signed by the Chief Executive Officer |
| Statutory auditors' audit | March and April | Statutory auditors |
| Approval of the accounts and management report for submission by the Board of Directors | 30 April | Board of Directors |
| Approval by the ordinary general meeting | 30 June | Shareholders |
| Filing with the registry and transmission to COSUMAF | In accordance with PC-PRO-REP-001 | Accounting; RCCI |

If the financial statements approved by the Board differ from those in the DSF, an amended return is filed.

### Article 29 — Approval of the accounts

The Chief Executive Officer presents to the Board of Directors the financial statements, the management report, the proposed appropriation of profit, the own funds position and the related-party agreements. The Board approves the accounts for submission and convenes the ordinary general meeting, which approves them within six months of the year end. The documents are made available to the statutory auditors and shareholders within the time limits of the Uniform Act; the minutes are kept in the register of resolutions.

### Article 30 — Statutory auditors

- The Chief Executive Officer is the statutory auditors' point of contact; Accounting provides them, without restriction, with the books, supporting documents and explanations requested.
- Their letter of representation is signed by the Chief Executive Officer after review by the Board.
- Their recommendations are entered in the recommendation tracking table of the Internal Control and Compliance Charter (PC-COD-003); the RCCI monitors their implementation.
- They are informed of significant differences on client assets and of any suspicion of fraud.
- Any engagement other than certification entrusted to the statutory auditors is authorised in advance by the Board of Directors.

## Title IX — Own funds and prudential monitoring

### Article 31 — Monthly calculation of own funds

At each monthly closing, Accounting calculates own funds using Appendix 5:

**Own funds** = paid-up capital + reserves + retained earnings + profit or loss for the current year at the calculation date (including any provisional loss) − subscribed capital not called − intangible assets and formation expenses − receivables from shareholders, directors and senior managers.

This internal definition is deliberately prudent. It is replaced by the COSUMAF definition as soon as the instruction applicable to brokerage firms is confirmed. The RCCI checks the calculation before it is circulated.

The following are calculated at the same time, in accordance with PC-POL-RIS-001: available own cash in months of fixed costs, the share of cash held with the largest bank, firm underwriting or standby commitments as a percentage of own funds, and the share of revenue from the largest client.

### Article 32 — Thresholds, alerts and transmission

| Situation | Time limit | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Own funds above the alert threshold | Monthly | Dashboard sent to the Chief Executive Officer and the risk and compliance committee before the 15th of the month |
| Alert threshold crossed (FCFA 300,000,000 plus three months of fixed costs) | 5 business days | Board of Directors informed, with a recovery plan (PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 13) |
| Regulatory minimum crossed (FCFA 300,000,000) | Without delay | Board and COSUMAF informed; shareholders convened to restore own funds |
| Other indicator in amber or red zone | In accordance with PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 27 | Escalation provided for by that policy |

Prudential returns due to COSUMAF are prepared by Accounting and sent through the workflow of PC-PRO-REP-001.

## Title X — Management, retention and fraud prevention

### Article 33 — Budget

Each year, the Chief Executive Officer prepares, with Accounting and the directors, the budget for the following year: revenue by business line, expenses, investments, expected cash and own funds. They present it to the Board of Directors at its last meeting of the year. The approved budget is the reference for committing expenditure (Article 15) and for the calculation of fixed costs under PC-POL-RIS-001.

### Article 34 — Reporting to the Board

Each quarter, the Chief Executive Officer presents to the Board of Directors a financial report prepared by Accounting:

- income statement for the quarter and year to date, compared with the budget, with an explanation of variances of more than 10%;
- cash and the twelve-month cash plan;
- own funds and the indicators in Appendix 5;
- overdue receivables and litigation;
- unbudgeted expenses and payments above the thresholds of PC-COD-004;
- profit forecast for the year.

### Article 35 — Retention

Accounting books, supporting documents, closing files, returns and reconciliations are retained for **ten years** from the end of the financial year, in electronic form, in accordance with PC-PRO-ARC-001. At the end of a chartered accountant's engagement, the Company recovers all its data before paying the balance of the fees.

### Article 36 — Fraud prevention

In addition to segregation of duties (Article 10), the Company applies the following rules:

1. **Dual validation** of every payment and telephone confirmation of any change of bank details (Articles 19 and 21).
2. **Caution about urgency**: an unusual or urgent payment request, even in the name of a senior manager, is verified with that person by voice before execution.
3. **Review of manual entries**: each month, the Chief Executive Officer goes through the list of manual entries on cash, revenue and client liability accounts.
4. **Supplier review**: each year, Accounting checks that each supplier paid exists and is not linked to an undeclared Staff Member.
5. **Leave**: the person who handles petty cash or payments takes at least one consecutive week of leave each year, during which another Staff Member replaces them.
6. **Whistleblowing**: any Staff Member who suspects fraud reports it through the whistleblowing system of the Code of Conduct (PC-COD-001, Article 47); they suffer no retaliation.

Any fraud or attempted fraud is handled as an incident in accordance with PC-POL-RIS-001, Article 28.

### Article 37 — Control

The RCCI checks at least: each month, the own funds calculation; each quarter, one month of reconciliations and a sample of payments; every six months, petty cash; each year, the tax and social security calendar and the fixed asset register. Their findings follow the Internal Control and Compliance Charter (PC-COD-003).

## Title XI — Entry into force

### Article 38 — Entry into force

This manual enters into force on the date of its approval, as shown in the document control sheet. It is reviewed each year, and upon any change in the accounting organisation, service provider, bank, or tax or prudential regulations.
